## Candidate patterns

### P1: AI sign-language interpreting is being marketed as a rural/low-access "backup" for human interpreters, built without Deaf leadership
- **What it is**: Companies (Nagish, Sorenson, UK startup Silence Speaks) are shipping or piloting real-time AI ASL-to-text/avatar interpreting explicitly framed as solving the shortage of human interpreters in rural or low-access settings — not as a Deaf-led tool.
- **Evidence**: Nagish's CEO frames the product's main value as helping people in rural areas "where interpreters are hard to get" (limpingchicken.com interview). Sorenson and Silence Speaks have unveiled AI-ASL translation avatars without a human interpreter in the loop. A global survey of Deaf/HoH community members on sign-language technologies found the top two concerns were "hearing people making money from sign language technologies" and "few deaf people leading or contributing" (themoonlight.io review of the survey). r/ASLinterpreters pushed back on an automated-interpreting timeline, with moderators urging people to lobby for certified human interpreters instead.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: No product found pairs the rural-access use case with Deaf-led design or co-governance; the gap is specifically a trustworthy, community-governed version of the same access promise, not the access promise itself.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found Deaf-led *governance frameworks and recommendations* (Cambridge/Minderoo report on BSL procurement calling for majority-Deaf governance; EUD's 15-principle ethical framework) plus two company-published commitments — DeepMind's "AI Sign Language Advisory Committee" (AISLAC) for its SL2T sign-to-text feature, and Signapse's published AI governance framework claiming Deaf involvement in design/testing/governance. These are self-reported company structures or third-party recommendations, not independently audited products that pair the rural-access pitch with verified Deaf co-governance — DeepMind's own feature is also still ASL-to-English only. GitHub search ("ASL interpreter AI", "sign language translation deaf-led") found only small hobbyist gesture-recognition repos, no governance-relevant hits. Narrow/self-reported/unevaluated → PARTIAL, not SATURATED.

### P2: DHH users route around the lack of ASL/Deaf-cultural grounding in general LLMs by using them as a text-only workaround
- **What it is**: Deaf and hard-of-hearing people are already using mainstream text-based LLM tools (ChatGPT, etc.) to ease communication, but these tools have no native ASL support and no Deaf-cultural grounding, so users must adapt the tool rather than the tool adapting to them.
- **Evidence**: An NSF-funded mixed-methods survey of 191 DHH ChatGPT users found real benefit from eased communication, but flagged "lack of American Sign Language (ASL) support and Deaf cultural understanding" and bias "rooted in non-representative datasets and assumptions by hearing researchers" (par.nsf.gov/biblio/10635776, "We Do Use How Hearing People Think").
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The paper documents the workaround and the gap as a research finding, not a shipped product addressing it; no tool found bakes ASL-aware or Deaf-cultural context into a general assistant.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch surfaced one working prototype: İnan et al. (NAACL 2024 industry track, Northeastern/Gallaudet, arXiv 2410.14026) built a goal-oriented dialogue system that generates ASL video instructions via LLM-based text-to-gloss translation, co-developed with Deaf/HoH community input, deployed on Alexa Echo Show for the Alexa Prize TaskBot Challenge, with public code/data. It's a narrow research task-assistant, not a general-purpose assistant with native Deaf-cultural grounding — GitHub search ("ASL chatbot deaf") returned zero relevant repos, and no mainstream commercial chatbot with Deaf-cultural grounding was found. Narrow/research-only → PARTIAL.

### P3: No consumer-facing AI tool audits a survivor's smart devices/cloud accounts for abuser-linked tracking after separation — it's still a manual advocate checklist
- **What it is**: Abusers increasingly exploit wearables, smart-home devices, and linked cloud accounts to keep tracking survivors after separation (e.g., a smartwatch left behind during a flight whose linked cloud account kept surveillance going even after the device was recovered). The only widely documented safety strategy is advocates manually telling clients to turn off phone location tracking.
- **Evidence**: Refuge (UK) reports a "significant rise" in abuse cases misusing wearables and AI, including the smartwatch/cloud-account case (familylaw.co.uk coverage). A 2026 SSWR conference study found that within most agencies, the only explicit tech-safety strategy taught to clients was disabling phone location tracking, and advocates self-reported feeling undereducated on tech-related safety planning; the same study found participants receptive to a proposed "Technology Abuse Clinic" model that would incorporate cybersecurity experts into safety planning (sswr.confex.com/sswr/2026/webprogram/Paper60221.html).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The "Technology Abuse Clinic" is explicitly a proposed service-staffing model requiring human cybersecurity experts on-call, not an AI tool; no product found does automated device/account-linkage auditing for survivors.
- **Maturity**: OPEN
- **Verdict notes**: GitHub search ("stalkerware detection", "tech abuse survivor safety audit") found multiple device-side stalkerware/malware scanners (GetByeBye, selfguard, Zero, StealthDetect, tsunamicodes/Stalkerware_detection, foundation-safe-harbor) — all scan a single phone for spyware apps/malware, none audit cross-account or smart-home/wearable cloud-linkage. WebSearch found the closest real matches are explicitly human-led: Nansen Digital Forensic Services' paid digital-safety audit and the Salvation Army's "Safer in the Home" remote tech health check (which does review Google/iCloud/OneDrive sharing and sign-in activity) are both advocate/professional-run, not self-service AI tools, and eSafety.gov.au's checklist is a manual worksheet. No automated AI tool found that audits a survivor's linked smart devices and cloud accounts for abuser-linked tracking. Genuine multi-channel search found only adjacent-but-narrower tools and manual processes → OPEN.

### P4: Survivors already ask LLMs about technology-facilitated abuse, but no safety-vetted assistant exists for this specific high-stakes use case
- **What it is**: General-purpose LLMs are already embedded in platforms where survivors seek informal help with technology-facilitated abuse (TFA) questions, but there is no assistant purpose-built and safety-evaluated for this use case.
- **Evidence**: An arXiv paper (2602.17672) evaluates LLM responses to survivor questions about TFA specifically because "LLMs are already integrated into widely used online platforms where survivors seek support," and because advocates are scarce and generic online information about TFA is often inaccurate or misleading; the paper's own evaluation axes are accuracy, completeness, safety, and actionability — framed as an open research question.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The paper is an evaluation of existing general-purpose LLMs against this use case, not a description of a dedicated, safety-reviewed product; no shipped "TFA-safe assistant" was found in this search.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found Refuge (UK) runs a live chatbot offering real-time automated tech-abuse support, designed with a PhD researcher at Central Saint Martins working directly with survivors and Refuge to shape and test it — but it's scoped narrowly to device-securing guidance, not general TFA Q&A. Chayn's "Little Window" chatbot ran a 3-year survivor-tested pilot before being taken down in 2020 over safety/sustainability concerns. The cited USENIX Security 2026 paper (Prakash et al., arXiv 2602.17672) evaluates four LLMs including "two models built for intimate partner violence contexts" specifically — meaning narrow-purpose models already exist, just not independently vetted as safe/complete/actionable at scale, and Refuge's own is narrow/single-purpose. GitHub search ("domestic violence chatbot safety assistant") returned no relevant repos. Narrow, discontinued, or unevaluated-at-scale implementations exist → PARTIAL, not OPEN.

### P5: Pesticide-safety and labor-rights information for farmworkers stays locked to apps/digital delivery, and "bilingual" means Spanish — not the language workers actually speak
- **What it is**: Updated bilingual (English/Spanish) pesticide-safety apps are shipping, but pesticide labels remain largely English-only, and digital/app-based delivery assumes reliable connectivity that employer-provided internet often doesn't guarantee.
- **Evidence**: University of Washington's PNASH program launched an updated bilingual pesticide-safety app for agricultural workers in Washington/Oregon in March 2026 (pnash.deohs.washington.edu). A 2025 commentary (foodsystemsjournal.org, "Implementing Spanish-language pesticide labeling") argues digital-only Spanish delivery won't reach every worker because employer-provided internet access is unreliable, leaving workers without information during disconnected periods; a cited 2018 Washington figure attributes most farmworker pesticide illnesses to English-only label instructions.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: No tool found addresses the connectivity gap (offline-first delivery) or the language gap below Spanish (see P6) in combination with real-time pesticide-safety guidance.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Follow-up WebSearch on PNASH's relaunched app (branded PestiSafe/PestiSeguro in coverage) found it is explicitly described as working offline in remote agricultural/forestry settings, in addition to being bilingual English/Spanish — meaning the connectivity-gap half of this pattern is already addressed by a real, actively-maintained (March 2026 relaunch, 2026 grant expanding its product database 100→2,000) implementation, contradicting the digest's original framing. "Pesticide Labels, Now!" is a second English/Spanish app on both app stores. What remains unaddressed is coverage below Spanish for indigenous-language speakers combined with offline delivery (see P6) — no tool found does both together. Existing implementation is real but narrow (stops at Spanish) → PARTIAL, not OPEN.

### P6: Indigenous-language-speaking farmworkers and parents are undercounted, underserved by human interpreters, and AI translation tools perform visibly worse for them than for Spanish speakers
- **What it is**: A large population of farmworkers and their families speak indigenous Mexican/Central American languages (Mixteco, Zapotec, Triqui) rather than Spanish, face a severe human-interpreter shortage, and the AI translation tools being deployed as a stopgap demonstrably degrade for these languages.
- **Evidence**: EdSource (March 2026) reports Triqui- and Mixteco-speaking parents in Greenfield, CA (Salinas Valley) saying they couldn't advocate for their children without interpreters; the district relies partly on Nurbli, an AI-based over-the-phone interpretation app, and advocates say there's a severe undercount of students who speak these languages (state data: 7,681 Mixteco speakers, 374 Zapotec speakers statewide in 2024-25). A September 2026 Mexican report (grupoanimal.mx) found Zapotec and Mixtec speakers testing chatbots got responses that repeated the same terms or flatly said "no entiendo" ("I don't understand") — a failure mode speakers said wouldn't occur in Spanish. An older Middlebury Institute piece notes 20,000+ Salinas Valley immigrants speak neither English nor Spanish, and that even specialized human-interpreter services (Indigenous Interpreting+) struggle to cover the many language variations.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every mitigation found (Nurbli, Indigenous Interpreting+) is a thin, partial stopgap explicitly described as struggling with coverage or quality for this population; no tool found closes the quality gap between Spanish and indigenous-language AI translation.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found Google Translate added Zapotec, Mixtec, and Nahuatl (2025 rollout) and ModelFront advertises "full support" for both Mixtec and Zapotec — i.e., shipped, maintained products exist. But academic MT literature on Mixtec-Spanish shows wildly inconsistent quality depending on domain/corpus (BLEU ~17-22 on a general corpus vs. ~96-99 on a narrow educational/religious-text corpus) and flags severe dialect fragmentation (INALI counts 81 Mixtec variants, 50+ Zapotec languages, often differing town-to-town) — exactly the kind of mismatch that would produce the "no entiendo" chatbot failures the digest's P6 evidence already documents for real users. GitHub search ("Mixtec Zapotec translation", "indigenous language machine translation low resource") returned zero results. Vendor coverage claims are unverified for agricultural/health vocabulary and don't squarely close the gap → PARTIAL, not SATURATED.
