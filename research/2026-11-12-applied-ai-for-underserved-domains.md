## Candidate patterns

### P1: Offline/on-device AI triage assistants for community health workers
- **What it is**: Lightweight LLMs (e.g., Gemma) running fully on-device (phone or clinic laptop, no internet) to help community health workers (CHWs) triage wounds, rashes, symptoms, and documents in low-connectivity clinics.
- **Evidence**: A solo-developer field report on dev.to ("Building medical AI for the other 90%") states every inference path must run on a phone CPU or district-clinic laptop with no internet, and several planned pieces are "not yet built." "Trij" is described as a free, open-source on-device PWA for CHW triage. "Sentinel Health" is a Gemma-based offline triage tool with a deterministic safety layer, built for the Gemma 4 Good Hackathon (Kaggle 2026) — hackathon-stage, not a shipped product. Tibbi (Pakistan) offers a "Health House Mode" offline dashboard for Lady Health Workers.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Most examples are solo side-projects or hackathon entries, explicitly incomplete per their own authors; an HLTH industry piece states there is "no requirement or incentive for developers to test AI tools in rural settings, include rural data in training sets, or design for low-bandwidth environments."
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: GitHub/web search found a real cluster of implementations — Trij (github.com/Mosss-OS/trij, PWA), Sentinel Health, PocketTriage, TriageOS, SHIFA (github.com/Mist-Labs/shifa), TriageMate, plus Google's MedGemma (a product, not a full CHW triage app) — but every community-built one is a hackathon entry or solo prototype, several explicitly self-labeled "unvalidated prototype v0.1," with no independent clinical evaluation found. Real gap for a serious, tested, maintained build remains.

### P2: LLM-generated synthetic data + human-in-the-loop validation to bootstrap low-resource-language models
- **What it is**: Instead of waiting for scarce native-language corpora, teams use a frontier LLM to generate synthetic text/instruction data in a target low-resource language, then have native speakers validate it, then LoRA-finetune an open-weight model on the result.
- **Evidence**: Konkani-Instruct-100k (community project, published on Hugging Face) used exactly this pipeline; TharuChat (arXiv, March 2026) had Gemini generate synthetic grammar/folklore data validated by humans, then released a LoRA finetune of LLaMA-3.2-3B; NagaNLP (arXiv) built an open-source toolkit for Nagamese using the same LLM-synthetic-then-human-validated approach; a 2026 ACL workshop ("Dataset Creation for Lower-Resourced Languages," DCLRL 2026) is dedicated to this problem class; a Kazakh instruction dataset and InstructLR framework follow the same pattern.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every team (Konkani, Tharu, Nagamese, Kazakh, Sinhala, Belarusian, Albanian) is re-inventing the same synthetic-data-plus-validation pipeline independently from scratch for one language at a time; no single reusable, general-purpose toolkit or workflow for "bootstrap any near-zero-resource language" was found in the search — each is a bespoke one-off repo/paper.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Search confirmed NagaNLP (arXiv 2512.12537) explicitly bills itself as "a reproducible framework for reducing data scarcity in other low-resource contexts," and mTIG (github.com/okalai-ai/mtig) is a reusable grammar-based synthetic-data generator; but no general-purpose, maintained, multi-language toolkit was found in actual use beyond single-language papers — each team (Konkani, Tharu, Nagamese, Kazakh) still re-implements the pipeline from scratch. Differentiated general tool remains viable.

### P3: Messaging-channel (WhatsApp/Telegram) AI agents for frontline health workers that stall at pilot stage
- **What it is**: LLM-powered chatbots delivered over WhatsApp/Telegram to give CHWs or patients medical information, reminders, and triage support, often in local languages.
- **Evidence**: ASHABot (India, documented in an arXiv paper and covered by Microsoft Research and CHW Central) is an LLM-powered, experts-in-the-loop WhatsApp chatbot for CHW information needs. Dimagi's "Open Chat Studio" is a general open platform for building such bots. "Aarogya Saathi" is a hobby project (per its dev.to writeup) built on OpenClaw, giving Hindi-language first aid guidance 24/7, but requires a server/internet connection.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: A February 2026 ICTworks piece cites that only 34% of these AI initiatives show any sign of scaling beyond pilot phase; a separate newsletter survey found 61% of health systems are "piloting" AI tools for CHW programs but only one reports "meaningful integration" nationally — the gap is specifically in sustained deployment/integration tooling, not the chatbot technique itself.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: ASHABot (CHI 2025/MSR, arXiv 2409.10913) completed a real two-month field deployment with 20 ASHAs, and Turn.io's "Chat for Health Accelerator 2026" (Anthropic as AI partner) is explicitly funding reusable open-source scaling assets — so well-resourced players are now targeting exactly this integration gap. But the accelerator only opened applications in 2026 and has no shipped output yet, and ASHABot itself remains a research pilot, not deployed integration tooling — gap still open for a build that closes the pilot-to-scale tooling layer specifically.

### P4: AI-assisted legal-aid chatbots paired with a mandatory human-verification bottleneck
- **What it is**: Nonprofit legal-aid organizations building narrow-scope AI chatbots (intake, plain-language guidance, form drafting) for immigration/benefits/domestic-worker cases, always backed by pro bono attorneys who manually re-verify AI output before it's relied on.
- **Evidence**: Lone Star Legal Aid's "Navi" and NC Legal Aid's "LIA" (24/7 public chatbot, built with pro bono K&L Gates lawyers on the back end) are LSC Technology-Initiative-Grant-funded projects; PoBot (Hong Kong, built by Linklaters Asia pro bono attorneys for the labor-rights group Migrasia) is explicitly described by a pro bono partner as still spending "much of the project's time... dedicated to verifying the AI's findings"; Microsoft's pro bono program described a DACA-renewal-form-automation Copilot tool for volunteers.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Across every example found, the verification step is manual and ad hoc (a lawyer re-checking AI output line by line) rather than a structured, auditable tool — no example surfaced of a dedicated citation-checking/verification-workflow layer purpose-built for non-expert legal-aid reviewers.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found multiple mature citation-verification products (Beyond Assured/Benchly, CiteSentinel, Clearbrief Cite Check Report, BriefCatch RealityCheck, CiteCheck AI/LawDroid) that solve structured AI-citation verification — but every one is marketed to law firms/courts for case-law citation checking, not adapted for non-expert legal-aid-nonprofit reviewers verifying plain-language intake/form-drafting output. The specific population's tooling gap is real and differentiable even though the underlying verification technique is commoditized elsewhere.

### P5: Voice-first, multilingual advisory agents for low-literacy users, built domain-by-domain instead of as a reusable pattern
- **What it is**: Voice-interface AI agents (not text/chat) that let low-literacy users in local dialects get advice — currently concentrated in agriculture (farmer advisory) but structurally the same interaction pattern other domains need.
- **Evidence**: Darli AI (Farmerline, Ghana) is a WhatsApp chatbot supporting 27 languages (20 African) claiming 110,000+ farmers reached (self-reported); Microsoft's "Project Gecko" targets smallholder farmers in Kenya/India with small models that run on cheap rural devices, building on Digital Green's FarmerChat; GSMA's EmergingTech cohort includes ToumAI (voice interface in Moroccan dialects for low-literacy rural users) and Wiseyak (multilingual conversational AI, Nepal).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: A WEF piece notes most AI chatbots are trained on only ~100 of the world's 7,000+ languages, and each of these voice-agent projects is a bespoke, org-specific, single-domain (mostly agriculture) build rather than a generalizable voice-first low-literacy interaction framework portable to health, legal, or civic domains.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Sarvam AI's production voice-agent guides already share one LiveKit-based pattern across government-services and agriculture verticals (docs.sarvam.ai), and an arXiv paper (2603.05743) proposes a general oral-first multi-agent architecture (demonstrated only on Guaraní). Everything else found (Mijke, Navana.ai, VoiceAid, Jan Sahayak) is single-domain or hackathon-stage. A reusable, portable, open framework spanning health/legal/civic beyond Sarvam's proprietary-API pattern was not found — real room for a differentiated, open build.

### P6: Low-bandwidth / field-condition stress-testing as a missing standard dev practice
- **What it is**: A systematic way to test whether an AI tool actually works under the bandwidth, device, and connectivity constraints of the underserved environment it targets, rather than assuming it will.
- **Evidence**: An HLTH industry article ("Why AI tools fail rural communities and how gen AI could fix it") states plainly there is no requirement or incentive for developers to test AI tools in rural settings, include rural data in training sets, or design for low-bandwidth environments; this complements the pilot-to-scale gap noted in P3 (34% scaling rate per ICTworks).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: No dedicated tool, checklist, or harness for "simulate rural bandwidth/device conditions and evaluate AI tool degradation" surfaced in search — this reads as an acknowledged gap in the field rather than a solved problem, called out directly by name in trade coverage.
- **Maturity**: OPEN
- **Verdict notes**: GitHub search for an AI-specific low-bandwidth/field-condition test harness returned nothing. WebSearch surfaced only generic, non-AI network-degradation tooling (Linux tc/netem via wondershaper, NectoProxy 2G/3G profiles, Chrome DevTools, Facebook's Augmented Traffic Control, BrowserStack/AWS Device Farm throttling) — commodity building blocks, not an assembled "evaluate AI tool degradation under rural bandwidth/device constraints" harness or checklist. No credible implementation of the actual pattern found after a genuine multi-angle search.

### P7: Independent impact/safety audit framework for "AI for Good" pilots (innovation-arbitrage critique)
- **What it is**: A structured evaluation process — analogous to a clinical-trial protocol — for vetting AI pilots before/while they're deployed on underserved communities, so those communities aren't used as unaccountable test grounds for unproven tech.
- **Evidence**: A Harvard Berkman Klein Center piece ("'AI for Good' shouldn't become the new innovation arbitrage") explicitly warns that AI-for-good initiatives can exploit under-resourced communities as test grounds for unproven tech; nonprofit-sector commentary (Nonprofit Quarterly, IDR Online) separately urges asking whether training data is representative of the community served and stresses transparency/equity/community input.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The critique is being made in commentary/advocacy pieces, not accompanied by any named, adopted audit framework or tool in the sources found — it's flagged as a live, unaddressed risk rather than a solved governance problem.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: J-PAL/Agency Fund's "AI evaluation framework for the development sector" (povertyactionlab.org) is a named, published four-level framework (model/product/user/impact evaluation) from a credible research org, and Singapore's SCAI Question 11 proposes a comparable adoption framework — but J-PAL's own framework is described as still "undergoing review," and no evidence of broad adoption or a usable audit tool/checklist in active use was found. A real framework exists but isn't mature or adopted; differentiated build/adoption-aid still viable.
