## Candidate patterns

### P1: Precision-routing layer for multimodal voice+screen agents (small on-screen detail → specialist CV model)
- **What it is**: In a voice agent that also watches the screen, route only the fine-grained visual subtasks (small text, dense UI, tiny icons) to a dedicated computer-vision model, rather than relying on the general-purpose multimodal model to catch everything, while the multimodal model continues to handle the voice+scene-level reasoning loop.
- **Evidence**: Getstream.io engineering blog explicitly recommends: "Route precision tasks (like small on-screen text) to a specialized CV model instead of expecting the multimodal model to catch everything." A Together AI-adjacent post (backlist.sdan.io) frames the emerging pattern as "voice agents get a lot more interesting when they can use the screen." A production-focused 2026 post (alexcloudstar.com) states the hardest part of multimodal agents "isn't any single modality — it's orchestrating them together."
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: All three sources are advisory/practitioner blog posts describing a recommended pattern, not naming or linking any shipped, reusable precision-routing middleware that plugs into an arbitrary voice+screen agent stack.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "multimodal voice agent" / "screen vision router voice" surfaced only hobby voice-agent repos with no precision-routing logic. WebSearch found NVIDIA's XR-AI conversation-example docs (nvidia.github.io/xr-ai) implementing a concrete router that sends direct visual questions to one model (Cosmos) and screens frames with another (Clef) — a real, working reference implementation, but explicitly a docs example, not a packaged/reusable library. An arXiv paper treats per-action VLM routing for computer-use agents as a cost/risk routing problem (research only). No reusable, product-grade middleware found; the one working example is narrow/reference-grade — meets the PARTIAL bar, not SATURATED.

### P2: Continuous affect-conditioned TTS/response generation (vs. discrete emotion-label classification)
- **What it is**: Instead of classifying user speech into a fixed emotion taxonomy (happy/sad/angry/etc.) and templating a response, condition the agent's reply and TTS prosody on a continuous valence-arousal signal inferred from the user's voice, updated turn by turn.
- **Evidence**: Sympatheia (arXiv 2606.00851) is a speech-to-speech system "conditioned on affect inferred from the user's speech" with continuous affect conditioning. AffAdapt (UIST 2026 adjunct) updates an embodied persona's affect state every turn. A VR study (arXiv 2603.09324, n=30) found 93.3% of participants preferred a prosody-driven emotion-aware agent. A CallSphere practitioner blog states the standard seven-basic-emotions taxonomy "is unreliable on phone audio."
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every working example is an academic paper or workshop demo with its own bespoke evaluation; the only production-facing claim found (VoiceGenie.ai "adapts tone and dialogue dynamically") is unverified marketing copy, not a documented, adopted implementation.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "affect aware TTS" returned zero hits; "emotion voice agent" returned two near-empty student repos. WebSearch confirmed a real but purely academic cluster: Sympatheia (LoRA-tuned on GLM-4-Voice, continuous VA input), EmoSteer-TTS (training-free activation steering, has code), CoCoEmo, and a PAD-based TTS framework — genuine, runnable implementations, but every one is a research artifact with its own author-built eval, none adopted or shipped. Narrow/unevaluated-for-production implementations exist, which is the PARTIAL bar, not OPEN.

### P3: Consolidated, broadly-adopted end-to-end voice-agent evaluation framework (audio-native + validated LLM-judge, not another one-off benchmark)
- **What it is**: A single evaluation framework that scores full multi-turn spoken conversations end-to-end (not per-component ASR/LLM/TTS metrics) and whose LLM-judge component has been independently validated for reliability, rather than each team building its own bespoke rubric.
- **Evidence**: ServiceNow's EVA paper states "most existing efforts test components in isolation." The EVA-Bench follow-up (arXiv 2605.13841) argues "no existing benchmark jointly addresses" realistic simulated conversation generation and full-scope failure-mode measurement. Ultravox's engineering blog says explicitly that "none [of the existing benchmarks] perfectly addresses our users' specific needs," so they built their own. A Sprinklr paper on LLM-judge reliability for voice agents finds judge reliability is "metric- and configuration-dependent rather than uniform," recommending human-in-the-loop hybrids.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The evidence is itself the gap — EVA, EVA-Bench, Cekura, Ultravox's custom benchmark, and DeepEval's tooling are five separate, non-converged efforts built in the same few months because none served as the adopted standard; fragmentation this dense this recently signals no consolidated tool has yet won.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "voice agent eval" turned up at least eight distinct, independent eval repos created in 2026 (ArtemisVoice, ai-voice-agent-eval-framework, VoiceAgentsEval, voice-agent-eval, etc.), none with meaningful star counts or cross-adoption — direct confirmation of fragmentation. WebSearch confirmed EVA-Bench (ServiceNow, open-sourced, audio-native + LLM/LALM-judge) is the most complete single effort found, plus Dialpad/REALM (diagnostic-only by its own authors' admission) and a DeepEval vendor roundup. No evidence any one of these is adopted beyond its own authors — many narrow, non-converged, real implementations is squarely PARTIAL, not SATURATED (no consolidated winner) and not OPEN (these are actual built tools, not just discussion).

### P4: Continuous, speaker-identified, multi-user multimodal memory across voice sessions (no clean session boundaries)
- **What it is**: A memory system for voice/multimodal agents that identifies individual speakers by voice (and face, if video is present) inside continuous full-duplex audio with no explicit "session start/end" signal, attributes memories to the correct person in multi-party conversations, and lets those memories be incrementally edited as preferences change.
- **Evidence**: EgoMem explicitly states "earlier memory agents assume known user identities, clear session boundaries, and text-only input, and those assumptions fail in full-duplex omnimodal use." M2A (arXiv 2602.07624) argues "most personalization is static, while users keep refining their preferences" and ships a dual-layer editable memory. VoxPolyMem targets multi-party speaker attribution across sessions, scoring 85.0 on its own VoxPolyBench (23.6 points above its strongest baseline).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every implementation found is a 2025–2026 research paper evaluated on its own author-built benchmark (EgoMem, M2A, VoxPolyMem, HARMONI); no production voice-agent platform (Vapi, Retell, LiveKit, Pipecat, etc.) was found claiming continuous speaker-identified, boundary-free, multi-user memory as a shipped feature.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` found real shipped-but-narrow tools: `alexwboles/voice-memory` and `alexwboles/callrecall-ai` (self-hosted, per-caller-brief memory, zero dependencies, MIT) — functioning single-maintainer implementations, but single-speaker/per-caller, not multi-party speaker-identified. WebSearch found Memories.ai's shipped multimodal speaker diarization (a transcription product, not a voice agent, "world's first" claim unverified marketing) and LiveKit+Speechmatics' production real-time diarization add-on (no persistent memory layer). VoxPolyMem/EgoMem/M2A/AFA remain research-only, no maintained repos found. Real but narrow/split-feature implementations exist on both the memory side and the diarization side, just never combined in a shipped product — PARTIAL, not OPEN.

### P5: Production-grade (not pilot-study) voice-first agentic browsing/navigation for blind and low-vision users
- **What it is**: A voice-driven agent that lets a blind or low-vision user navigate the web (or arbitrary software UI) through natural conversation instead of a traditional screen reader's linear/keyboard-driven model, handling tasks like online shopping end-to-end by voice.
- **Evidence**: CUNY's "Screen Agent" (BARS 2026) is "presented as a pilot study." A CHI '26 Wizard-of-Oz study with 12 blind/severely-low-vision Korean adults testing a voice-based shopping agent found users "worried about hallucinations and about handing payment decisions to the agent," favoring "tightly constrained interactions." Google Research's Feb 2026 post frames StreetReaderAI and an audio-description video player explicitly as "prototypes, not shipped products." An accessibility essay (aaif.io) argues agent interfaces can create *new* barriers for screen-reader users rather than removing old ones.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every source found is explicitly labeled a pilot, prototype, or Wizard-of-Oz study by its own authors — none describes an installable, production-maintained tool, and the CHI study surfaces trust/hallucination concerns that an unbuilt safety layer would need to address.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "blind voice navigation agent" / "accessibility voice agent" returned zero hits. WebSearch found CoBrowse AI, a real beta product on AppleVis (voice-driven browser navigation for blind/low-vision users) explicitly working "only on websites where it's implemented" and with AppleVis itself cautioning the beta is no guarantee of public release, plus two hackathon project pages (Echo, Diamond Access AI) and the CUNY/CHI/Google prototypes already in the digest. One genuine narrow/beta implementation (CoBrowse AI) plus hackathon demos meets the PARTIAL bar (demo/narrow/unlaunched), not OPEN — though it is close to the OPEN line given no production-maintained tool exists.

### P6: Fully open-source, self-hosted, end-to-end real-time speech-to-speech translation agent
- **What it is**: A voice translation agent where ASR, translation, and TTS all run on self-hostable open-weight models end-to-end — no proprietary hosted API in the critical path — comparable in latency/quality to proprietary options.
- **Evidence**: OpenAI's `gpt-realtime-translate` (May 2026, 70+ languages in / 13 out, $0.034/min) is the strongest end-to-end option found, and it is fully proprietary/hosted. A Cerebras cookbook example chains LiveKit + Whisper + a Cerebras-hosted LLM + OpenAI TTS — explicitly "not fully self-hosted." A GMI Cloud guide chains Whisper, LLaMA 3, XTTS, and Gradio but runs the models on GMI's hosted endpoints. The 2026 ACL demo paper WIGVO ships open eval scripts and anonymized call logs, but is a research demo, not a deployable agent.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Across every source found, the fully open, self-hosted, end-to-end path either doesn't exist or runs partially on a hosted endpoint; the only genuinely end-to-end strong option is proprietary.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "speech to speech translation" surfaced `PolyTalkIO/polytalk` (AGPL-3.0, "privacy-first, self-hosted real-time speech-to-speech translation," Dockerized faster-whisper STT + Piper TTS + self-hostable translation backend) — a real, genuinely self-hostable, no-hosted-API-required implementation, contradicting the digest's "doesn't exist" framing. However, the project's own developers state on Lemmy that end-to-end latency is ~2s (not sub-200ms as a directory listing claimed), it's single-maintainer, and it's a cascade rather than a jointly-trained end-to-end model. Meta's open SeamlessM4T models were also cited as a self-hostable (if not agent-packaged) option. A real, working, self-hosted implementation exists but is narrow/early/unverified at scale — PARTIAL, not OPEN; not SATURATED since nothing self-hosted matches proprietary (gpt-realtime-translate) quality/latency.

### P7: Parallel (sub-turn-budget) multi-agent dispatch/handoff for voice, as opposed to serial triage-then-specialist handoff
- **What it is**: Instead of a single triage agent sequentially deciding which specialist to hand a call to (adding a full LLM round-trip of latency before the right agent even starts), dispatch to multiple candidate specialist agents in parallel and arbitrate/cancel, keeping the whole decision inside the voice turn's latency budget.
- **Evidence**: FutureAGI's 2026 multi-agent voice post states "serial dispatch to several workers blows the per-turn budget, so parallel variants are preferred for voice." An independent practitioner blog (craftedbydaniel.com) describes a custom-built "voice router that refuses to think" using "pattern-first multiagent orchestration for subsecond latency." LiveKit's own handoff-pattern writeup describes the mainstream approach — triage agent classifies intent, then hands off — as serial. Simbavoice notes the orchestration layer "becomes its own product" and testing burden grows with every added agent.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The parallel-dispatch pattern was found in exactly one individual practitioner's bespoke blog post, not in any vendor SDK; OpenAI's Agents SDK, Vapi, and Trillet's Crews all ship the serial triage-then-handoff pattern as their documented default.
- **Maturity**: OPEN
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "voice agent handoff" / "multi agent voice orchestration" found only serial/graph-based orchestration repos (e.g. `haytham042/Multi-Agent-Voice-Orchestration-System` using LangGraph routing), none implementing sub-turn-budget parallel dispatch with arbitration/cancellation. WebSearch confirmed the pattern lives in exactly one practitioner's bespoke blog (craftedbydaniel.com) plus vendor commentary (FutureAGI) recommending it in theory; a CallSphere benchmark that actually tried enabling parallel tool calls found it cut p50 latency only 18% (not the expected 60%) and increased wasted calls, so they disabled it in production. OpenAI Agents SDK, LangGraph, and CrewAI all document serial handoff as their default. Only discussion, one practitioner's hack, and one benchmark showing mixed/negative results — no credible shipped implementation. OPEN.

### P8: Tight end-to-end voice-to-motor-action models for robots (vs. bolted-together VLM + hand-tuned controller)
- **What it is**: A model that takes spoken instruction + live vision and directly produces robot motor commands in one learned pipeline, instead of today's common pattern of an LLM/VLM doing high-level reasoning that is then handed to a separately-engineered (often fuzzy-logic) low-level controller.
- **Evidence**: A 2026 arXiv paper on voice-driven robotic arm control "still pairs its vision-language model with a separate fuzzy-logic controller." An industry overview notes vision-language-action (VLA) models are described as unifying perception, language, and motor control "into a single architecture," but the robotic-arm paper's own implementation contradicts that in practice. A July 2026 preprint argues auditory learning is "increasingly important for embodied intelligence" yet remains comparatively under-explored versus vision and language. An industry piece separately flags touch/haptics and real-world voice sensing, not vision, as the hardest remaining sensing problems for embodied agents.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The paper most directly on-topic (voice-driven arm control) is itself evidence of the gap — its own authors fall back to a hand-engineered controller rather than genuine end-to-end voice→action learning, and the auditory modality is explicitly called out as under-explored relative to vision/language in embodied AI.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: `gh search repos` for "voice robot control" / "vision language action robot" surfaced many repos, but they split cleanly into two non-overlapping groups: simple voice-command robot controllers (Whisper/Porcupine → discrete commands, no learned motor-action fusion) and VLA manipulation repos (SmolVLA, Pi0 reimplementations, LeRobot-based) that take text, not voice, as the language channel — none combine both as the pattern requires. WebSearch found a genuine counter-example: ELLSA (ICLR 2026) is a real full-duplex, single-architecture model spanning vision/text/speech/action that reportedly matches modality-specific baselines on speech-interaction and manipulation benchmarks; RoboOmni and VITA-E are similar research-stage fusions. These are real, working implementations but research-only, author-evaluated, and not found packaged for reuse — PARTIAL, not OPEN, and far from SATURATED (no production robot platform found using this).
