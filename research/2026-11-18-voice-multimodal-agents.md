## Candidate patterns

### P1: Backchannel-vs-real-interruption classification still breaks in shipped products
- **What it is**: Voice agents must tell a filler sound ("mm-hmm", a cough, background noise) apart from a genuine attempt by the caller to take the floor, then decide whether to cut playback immediately. Vendors frame this as an architecture problem (a parallel classifier pipeline), not a single tunable setting.
- **Evidence**: Quiq's engineering blog is titled "why the interruption problem is architecture" (quiq.com, via WebSearch). A live Retell AI community bug report describes a production agent that "speaks the remainder of its sentence as a separate turn after barge-in" (community.retellai.com/t/agent-speaks-the-remainder-of-its-sentence-as-a-separate-turn-after-barge-in/3580, via WebSearch). A secondhand summary of r/AI_Agents builder discussion reports "interruption handling breaks more often than the conversation itself" and that naive VAD "treats every cough or background noise as interruption," with a poster asking "how others handle barge-in and false-interrupt filtering on mobile" (via WebSearch aggregator summary, unconfirmed against the original Reddit thread).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Even vendors selling managed voice-agent platforms (Retell) have live, open bug reports on this exact failure mode as of November 2026, and builders are still asking each other for mitigations rather than pointing at a settled solution.
- **Maturity**: SATURATED
- **Verdict notes**: Multiple well-resourced vendors ship dedicated backchannel/barge-in classifiers today: LiveKit Agents' "adaptive interruption handling" runs a post-VAD classifier with a configurable `backchannel_boundary` cooldown (docs.livekit.io); Telnyx ships `interrupt_prediction_threshold` as a shipped release-note feature (telnyx.com/release-notes); AssemblyAI's voice-agent guide documents a rule-based filler-word/grace-period filter. A 2022 Duplex Conversation paper (arXiv 2205.15060) already built an end-to-end classifier showing 89% of rule-based "interrupts" were false positives. The Retell community bug report shows execution quality still varies per vendor, but the architecture pattern itself is shipped and maintained by at least three different well-resourced teams, not merely discussed.

### P2: p99-style latency metrics don't track perceived responsiveness
- **What it is**: Teams instrument voice agents with standard latency percentiles (p50/p99), but a lower p99 doesn't guarantee a voice agent *feels* faster, because real-world call latency is reported as bimodal — most turns are fast, but a long tail (p95) dominates what a caller actually perceives.
- **Evidence**: A secondhand WebSearch summary cites a practitioner report titled "Voice Agent With 280ms p99 Felt Slower Than Competitor's 450ms" and another describing "real-world latency is bimodal (median ~800ms, p95 ~2.4s)" with the claim that p95, not the median, determines user perception (via WebSearch aggregator, aiweekly.co/bittide.aicompass.dev summaries of apparent r/AI_Agents discussion — unconfirmed against original thread).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The complaint is specifically that standard observability dashboards (p50/p99) are the wrong lens for voice UX, and no perception-aligned latency metric or tooling surfaced in any search — teams are naming the mismatch, not pointing at a fix.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Observability vendors (Cekura, Retell, Future AGI) all ship standard percentile dashboards (P50/P90/P95/P99) and Future AGI explicitly recommends tracking a single "user-perceived gap" metric (end-of-speech to first audible audio) rather than p99 — i.e. practitioners are converging on a fix, but no product or OSS tool was found that reports a bimodal/perception-aligned score as a first-class metric. WebSearch for `"bimodal latency" voice agent dashboard` and `perceived responsiveness score voice agent` surfaced only blog commentary, no shipped tool. Real gap, but adjacent tooling (percentile breakdowns, per-stage tracing) means a build here would be a metric/UX layer on existing infra, not greenfield.

### P3: Long-call context/memory degradation patched with ad hoc summarization
- **What it is**: As a voice call runs long, latency degrades turn-over-turn because the context window fills up (slowing the LLM) and memory/GC pressure rises; the common mitigation builders describe is manually inserting conversation summarization to condense old turns rather than using any built-in platform feature.
- **Evidence**: A Hugging Face forum thread titled "Voice agent latency degrades after turn 7-8 despite fixed system prompt, limited history — looking for mitigation ideas beyond what we've already tried" (discuss.huggingface.co/t/178232, via WebSearch) documents a practitioner actively stuck on this. A related blog note states the suggested mitigation is "conversation summarization (condense old turns)" as a workaround, not a platform feature.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The forum poster explicitly asks for ideas "beyond what we've already tried," i.e. existing mitigations were insufficient, and the fix found elsewhere is a manual technique rather than an adopted library or platform setting.
- **Maturity**: SATURATED
- **Verdict notes**: Pipecat (widely-used, maintained OSS voice-agent framework) ships built-in, non-blocking context summarization triggered at ~8000 tokens or 20 messages (docs.pipecat.ai/pipecat/fundamentals/context-summarization), and OpenAI's own cookbook ships a reference implementation for the Realtime API (developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/context_summarization_with_realtime_api). Decagon markets "context compaction" as a product feature and Squadstack's blog claims to have shipped a fix ("why voice AI gets worse the longer it talks, and how we fixed it"). The HF forum poster being still stuck suggests execution gaps persist for some teams, but the mitigation itself — summarization/compaction — is now a standard, maintained, documented feature in at least two major frameworks, not an ad hoc hack.

### P4: Multimodal voice agents lack visual grounding for deictic references
- **What it is**: When a multimodal voice+vision agent's caller/user says something like "this screen" or "the red button on the left," the agent frequently has no mechanism to resolve what is actually being pointed at, because orchestrating voice and vision together (not any single modality) is the hard part.
- **Evidence**: A WebSearch-surfaced engineering post states plainly that "when callers say 'this screen' or 'the red button on the left,' the agent has zero visual grounding" and that "the hardest part of multimodal agents isn't any single modality — it's orchestrating them together." A 2026 arXiv paper (abs/2602.05671, via WebSearch) concludes that "so long as multimodal voice agents cannot produce environmentally occasioned vision-based actions, they will lack a key resource relied upon by human remote sighted assistants" on physical inspection tasks.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Both a practitioner blog and a 2026 academic paper independently name the same gap from different angles (production orchestration vs. academic benchmark), and the practitioner-side mitigation offered is only a narrow workaround (route small on-screen text to a separate specialized CV model), not a general solution to deictic grounding.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Only academic work turned up, no production system: a March 2026 arXiv paper introduces EgoPoint-Ground (egocentric deictic-grounding dataset) with a baseline model SV-CoT, and a related EcoG benchmark paper argues existing benchmarks skip deictic reasoning. A 2025 Imperial College paper resolves "put that there" via gaze+pointing but for virtual windows, not screen UI. GitHub search for voice+vision deictic grounding and WebSearch for commercial products returned nothing beyond the narrow practitioner workaround already cited in the digest (routing small on-screen text to a separate CV model). Genuine open research gap with no converged solution, but enough recent academic activity (2+ 2026 papers) that this isn't untouched — PARTIAL, not OPEN.

### P5: Text-conditioned self-voice filtering as a new layer attacking the interruption problem
- **What it is**: Soniox's STT v6 (released 2026-11-03) takes the voice agent's own outgoing text as an extra input signal and filters the agent's own voice out of the live transcript, attacking the interruption-detection problem at the transcript layer rather than the audio/echo-cancellation layer that most other vendors use.
- **Evidence**: last30days engine's first run surfaced this via a Nov 8 voice-AI weekly roundup (voiceaispace.com, cited by the engine's WebSearch-equivalent synthesis pass): "Soniox released STT v6, which takes the voice agent's own text as input and filters the agent's voice out of transcripts" (November 3, 2026).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: This is a single just-shipped vendor feature (Nov 3, 2026) — no second implementation of this specific text-conditioned approach turned up in any search, and competing vendors (per the P1/P4 searches) still rely on device-level acoustic echo cancellation instead.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Confirmed single-vendor, just-shipped (Nov 3, 2026) feature via Soniox's own blog/marktechpost coverage — no competitor (Speechmatics, AssemblyAI, Deepgram) was found implementing text-conditioned self-voice filtering; Soniox's own docs admit "some agent audio still leaks through, especially at the start of speech," and a secondhand test report notes no real-device testing had been done yet. No independent benchmark exists for v6. This is exactly the PARTIAL profile: real implementation, but narrow (one vendor), unevaluated (no third-party benchmark), and acknowledged-incomplete by the vendor itself.

### P6: Voice-agent evaluation/observability is still hand-built per team
- **What it is**: Teams shipping voice agents build their own two-phase evaluation pipeline (offline simulation plus production tracing of STT→LLM→TTS→tool spans) because no single adopted standard framework exists yet for this.
- **Evidence**: Coval's blog states "until recently, there was no widely adopted voice AI testing framework" (coval.dev/coval.ai, via WebSearch). Multiple vendor docs (Pipecat's own Evals guide, Future AGI's write-up describing an OpenInference span tree covering STT/LLM/TTS/tools/audio) each describe a bespoke version of the same two-phase pattern rather than pointing to one converged-on standard.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The vendor framing itself ("no widely adopted... framework," "most teams skip it") is a direct admission of the gap, and every example found is either a different vendor's proprietary pipeline or a differently-shaped open tool, not a converged standard.
- **Maturity**: SATURATED
- **Verdict notes**: Multiple maintained OSS tracing/eval tools now cover exactly this pipeline: Arize Phoenix ships `openinference-instrumentation-pipecat`, an auto-instrumentor with per-turn STT/LLM/TTS spans, documented directly in Pipecat's own Evals guide (docs.pipecat.ai/pipecat/evals/platforms/arize); Future AGI's traceAI ships standalone OSS packages for both LiveKit and Pipecat with a full STT/LLM/tool/TTS span tree; a PyPI package `voiceeval-sdk` offers one-call OpenTelemetry instrumentation plus latency/cost breakdowns. Vendor framing ("no widely adopted standard") is accurate in that none of these has become *the* default, but the underlying gap — teams having nothing to instrument with and hand-rolling tracing from scratch — is closed by at least two actively maintained open-source projects (Arize Phoenix, traceAI).
