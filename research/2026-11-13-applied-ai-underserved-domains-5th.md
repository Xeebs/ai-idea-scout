### P1: Applicant-facing, self-service AI benefits navigator for SNAP/Medicaid (not just caseworker-facing)
- **What it is**: An AI tool that helps a benefits applicant themselves — not the caseworker processing their file — understand eligibility, fill out SNAP/Medicaid forms, and interpret confusing notices, usable without going through an agency intermediary.
- **Evidence**: Every open/notable project found is explicitly caseworker-side: Nava Labs' "Form-Filling Assistant" and "Caseworker Empowerment Toolkit" (navapbc.com), Code for America + Anthropic's MCP-based "SNAP Policy Navigator" built to give caseworkers "real-time access to verified federal, state, and county SNAP guidance" (edtechinnovationhub.com, statescoop.com), and Pyx Health's Medicaid/SNAP navigator aimed at compliance outreach (hmpgloballearningnetwork.com) (WebSearch: `"I wish there was" AI tool SNAP Medicaid benefits application navigator open source`). SNAP Screener is the one applicant-facing bot found, but it only answers pre-application eligibility questions, not form completion.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: A California pilot of Nava's caseworker-side tool reported a 73% reduction in application completion time — a large, credible win — yet that benefit flows to agency staff, not applicants, who still face the same end-to-end application burden alone. No search result showed an open, applicant-controlled equivalent.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Searched GitHub (no matching repos for "benefits navigator applicant SNAP Medicaid", "SNAP eligibility chatbot", "medicaid application assistant AI") and web. Found only narrow/experimental implementations: SNAP Screener (applicant-facing but eligibility-only, no form completion), Propel's documented tests of general-purpose agents (ChatGPT Agent, Claude Cowork) successfully starting SNAP applications on a user's behalf in CA/FL (propel.app, one-off experiment not a shipped product), and CDT noting document-based pre-filling is technically possible but not found deployed at scale. No dedicated, shipped applicant-controlled full-navigator product exists.

WebSearch found no AI-specific interoperability implementation: the only cross-jurisdiction data-sharing effort located is IJIS Institute's standards-based offender-information-sharing pilot (Illinois/Iowa/Tennessee), which is not AI-driven. ### P3: Applicant-side counter-tool against AI hiring/screening exclusion for disabled and formerly-incarcerated candidates
- **What it is**: A tool that helps a job applicant with a disability or conviction history detect, understand, or contest an AI hiring-screen's likely rejection reason, rather than only employer-side "bias audit" tooling.
- **Evidence**: Vera Institute (Oct 2026, vera.org) warns AI hiring tools are "filtering job applications before they ever reach a human reviewer" and "reinforcing barriers for formerly incarcerated people." AEI's April 2026 analysis separately notes AI-driven short-term employment losses concentrated among disabled workers in automation-prone roles, alongside one positive counter-example (a Chinese delivery firm's AI system narrowing a hearing-impaired courier wage gap from 8% to 3%) (WebSearch: `AI for disability employment, elder care, refugee resettlement, prisoner reentry November 2026`). Microsoft's accessibility blog names Mentra as addressing AI hiring bias specifically for neurodivergent candidates — an employer-side fix, not an applicant-side one.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Every mitigation found (Mentra, OECD's governance recommendations) is aimed at employers or policymakers; no search result surfaced a tool that works from the applicant's side of a screen they have no visibility into.
- **Maturity**: OPEN

Workday class-action suit, ACLU's FTC complaint against a personality-test vendor, a HireVue complaint from a Deaf Indigenous applicant, and EEOC ADA guidance — with one commentary explicitly noting proposed law "does not require... any mechanism for a rejected applicant to challenge an algorithmic outcome." No software tool that helps an applicant detect or contest a specific AI screening rejection was found; every mitigation is employer- or policymaker-facing (Mentra, OECD governance recs).

### P4: Independent, rigorous evaluation layer for elder-care AI companion loneliness claims
- **What it is**: A third-party, methodologically rigorous evaluation effort (not vendor-sponsored) that tracks and benchmarks AI companion/robot claims about reducing elder loneliness and depression, as a check against marketing statistics.
- **Evidence**: A 2026 meta-analysis found social robots do reduce loneliness in older adults, with bigger effects in institutional settings in Japan/Turkey than the US (ifp.nyu.edu). A December 2025 nursing-home feasibility study of the voice companion "Meela" showed improved depressive/anxiety symptoms but on only 23 completers, with authors explicitly calling for larger controlled trials (skillednursingnews.com). Against that thin base, the widely-cited "95% reduction in loneliness" figure for ElliQ traces to a state-agency press release describing a self-selected, screened group, not a controlled result — a gap a critical commentary (tilacum.vercel.app) explicitly calls out (WebSearch: `AI companion elder loneliness nursing home unverified claims no clinical evaluation 2026`).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The strongest, most-repeated public claim in this space is traceably unverified by the trials that exist, and two relevant clinical trials in the registry search were Withdrawn or not-yet-recruiting — there is no running, independent benchmark holding vendor claims accountable.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found genuine independent (non-vendor-funded) evaluations already exist — a Gerontechnology journal paper on ElliQ noting no prior systematic evaluation existed before its own work, a JMIR Aging review showing mixed/null results across Pepper/AIBO/Paro studies (including two conflicting accounts of the same UK Pepper trial), and a 2023 Frontiers paper on deception risk. These are real but fragmented, small-sample, one-off academic studies — not a sustained, running benchmark that continuously holds vendor claims (e.g., ElliQ's unreplicated self-reported "95.9%" figure) accountable, so the gap is real but not untouched.

### P6: Parent-facing IEP compliance/advocacy checker (vs. teacher-facing IEP-goal generators that already exist)
- **What it is**: A tool that helps a parent of a child with a disability independently verify whether a proposed IEP meets their state's specific IDEA compliance requirements and flag where it falls short — distinct from the teacher-facing tools that draft IEP goals in the first place.
- **Evidence**: Vendor tools like MagicSchool, Gemini EDU/Copilot workflows, and OpenEduCat's accommodation generator are all teacher-facing: they draft goals/accommodations from present-levels data for the teacher to review (WebSearch: `special education IEP accommodation AI tool parents teachers "I wish" "duct tape" generate`). The one explicit compliance caveat found — "teachers should review all generated goals against their state's specific IEP format requirements... IDEA compliance standards are implemented differently across states" — is aimed at the teacher checking their own output, not at an independent party checking the school's.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The search found zero parent-facing or advocacy-facing tools; every result assumed the school-side professional is the user, leaving the parent (who has the least IDEA-compliance expertise and the most at stake) with no equivalent tool.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found a real parent-facing implementation: **SENTINEL·IEP** (sentineliep.pages.dev) transcribes IEP meetings and flags statements that may conflict with IDEA/FAPE/FERPA, with timestamped flags. However its own disclaimer says it doesn't determine violations (defers to agencies/courts/attorneys), it runs on free Cloudflare Pages hosting consistent with an indie/demo-scale project, its "multi-AI" accuracy claims are unverified, and it's built around federal IDEA rather than state-specific requirements. GitHub search for "IEP compliance checker parent" and "IEP advocacy tool" returned no results. A separate iTherapy SBIR Phase I prototype (ies.ed.gov) targets compliance-checking but is educator-facing, not parent-facing.
