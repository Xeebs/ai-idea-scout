## Candidate patterns

### P1: Offline-first quantized LLMs deployed directly on frontline health workers' own phones
- **What it is**: Running a small/quantized clinical model (e.g., MedGemma) locally on an Android phone a community health worker already owns, instead of a cloud API that assumes reliable connectivity.
- **Evidence**: Crane AI Labs' EaseHealth runs an offline MedGemma deployment for health workers in rural Uganda (Google Health AI Developer Foundations showcase, found via WebSearch "offline AI model rural clinic community health worker ... evaluation benchmark"). The same source frames "the device gap" — most health workers' phones lacking sufficient RAM to run these models — as the primary barrier to scaling.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The showcase itself names the hardware constraint as unsolved; this is one named pilot deployment, not a general framework/toolchain for packaging and quantizing clinical models to fit sub-spec rural phones.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch found multiple independent pilots/proofs-of-concept (Crane AI's EaseHealth in Uganda — 268 health workers, 5-day field test; PRANAM/ApnaVaidya MLX-Swift iPhone PoC; AfyaPack personal-project RAG app; Breno Alves's Gemma-4 rapid-test-reader PoC; MAM-AI Zanzibar research prototype; AMEGA on-device benchmark study) but every one is a narrow, single-org pilot or unvetted preprint/blog, and each independently flags phone RAM/device compatibility as the unsolved blocker. No GitHub repo or product found offering a reusable toolchain for packaging/quantizing clinical models to sub-spec rural phones — gap is real, differentiation is open.

### P2: Locally-validated clinical safety benchmarks for LLMs in low-resource primary care
- **What it is**: A benchmark scoring LLM triage/diagnostic output against local clinicians' judgment for a specific low-resource region, rather than a generic medical QA benchmark built on Western clinical data.
- **Evidence**: The IyàwóBench preprint states "no validated benchmark exists" for evaluating LLM clinical triage reasoning in West African primary health settings (via WebSearch on rural clinic evaluation benchmarks); it cites a Kenyan evaluation across 16 primary care facilities where 7.8% of AI-generated management recommendations were "actively harmful." Separately, a Rwanda community-health-worker study (Nature Health, Feb 2026; dataset of 5,609 questions from 101 CHWs) explicitly flags that it does not assess multi-step reasoning chains, calling that "an area for future work."
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The preprint's own framing is the gap statement — the authors built one narrow benchmark precisely because none existed, and it has not generalized into standard practice across regions/languages.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: WebSearch surfaced three independent region-specific efforts (Rwanda CHW benchmark, 5,609 Kinyarwanda questions, published Nature Health Feb 2026; Kenya "Alama Health QA" RAG-grounded methodology paper, Swahili+English; IyàwóBench Nigeria febrile-triage arXiv preprint) — each is a real implementation but single-region, preprint-stage or newly-peer-reviewed, and none has generalized into a reusable cross-region standard. No GitHub tooling found. Narrow/unevaluated implementations exist, so PARTIAL rather than OPEN.

### P3: Volunteer-correction-in-the-loop data pipelines for low-resource-language model training
- **What it is**: Routing live human-to-human interactions (interpreters, teachers) through a model, then having volunteers correct the output and feeding those corrections back as training data, instead of scraping for low-resource-language corpora.
- **Evidence**: Tarjimly connects refugees with volunteer interpreters and uses the corrected conversations as machine-translation feedback (MacArthur Foundation grantee story, via WebSearch). Quill.org "leverages volunteers to correct data that trains LLMs for 55 low-resourced languages" (same search run).
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Each example is a single org's bespoke internal pipeline; no shared or open tooling lets a smaller nonprofit stand up the same "capture correction → retrain" loop for its own under-covered language without rebuilding it from scratch.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Found one genuine open-source implementation, CMULAB (`neulab/cmulab` on GitHub, 15 stars, last pushed Sep 2025) — a CMU human-in-the-loop NLP framework with OCR/fine-tuning correction loops, validated on Seneca. It's small and academic, not nonprofit-oriented tooling. Tarjimly and Quill.org confirmed as bespoke internal pipelines with no released tooling. Masakhane and Mozilla Common Voice are adjacent (volunteer-driven, open) but don't implement the correction-loop retraining pattern itself. Real but narrow/unadopted implementation exists → PARTIAL.

### P4: Meeting users on existing low-tech channels (WhatsApp, voice calls) instead of building new apps
- **What it is**: Putting the LLM behind a channel communities already use and trust — WhatsApp, a phone call, SMS — rather than a dedicated app that assumes a smartphone, app-store access, and literacy.
- **Evidence**: GiveDirectly used Proto to bring an AI assistant to rural Rwanda in Kinyarwanda over WhatsApp (via WebSearch); Khushi Baby with Microsoft Research India built a WhatsApp chatbot supporting ASHA community health workers; Jacaranda Health (Kenya) lets low-literacy women call an AI help desk on low-tech phones for maternal/newborn questions (via WebSearch "applied AI underserved communities subreddit ... low-resource languages").
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Each is a one-off integration built by/for a single nonprofit's existing channel; there is no reusable "channel adapter" that lets any org plug a domain-specific LLM into WhatsApp/voice without re-solving the same integration work.
- **Maturity**: SATURATED
- **Verdict notes**: Verified two actively-maintained, shipped open-source products that squarely close this gap: Dimagi's Open Chat Studio (`dimagi/open-chat-studio`, 48 GitHub stars, pushed same day as this check) — a no-code multi-LLM chatbot builder deploying to WhatsApp, Telegram, SMS and web; and Glific (`glific/glific`, 223 stars, pushed same day) — a nonprofit-focused open-source WhatsApp platform that integrates with Jugalbandi for LLM capability. Both are exactly the reusable "channel adapter" the pattern claims doesn't exist, maintained by well-resourced orgs (Dimagi, Tech4Dev).

### P6: Grounded-claim verification layer bolted onto general medical LLMs for low-resource deployment
- **What it is**: Middleware that checks a general-purpose medical model's output against clinical guidelines and flags "grounded vs. ungrounded" claims before a non-specialist frontline worker acts on it.
- **Evidence**: A solo developer's field report, "Building Medical AI for the Other 90%" (DEV Community, found via that exact WebSearch query), describes building this: a shared routing/citation layer with "a check that flags grounded versus ungrounded claims," built explicitly because the author concluded permissively-licensed public medical AI models are "not deployment-ready" for this population as-is.
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: A single solo developer's in-progress project — the report-orchestration layer is described as finished, but other modules are still pending internal validation; not shipped, independently tested, or adopted.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL
- **Verdict notes**: Several research-stage grounding/verification tools exist for medical LLMs generally — VerifAI (open-source biomedical RAG + post-hoc claim verification, full QA pipeline rather than a drop-in checker), OpenGATE (evidence-grounding checker whose own docs state its claim-extraction gate is "currently failing"), GroundCheck (a demo, not a library), and DIASENTINEL (a diabetes-specific multi-agent grounding system, code availability unconfirmed). None is a general, bolt-on grounded-claim layer specifically built for low-resource/non-specialist frontline deployment on top of permissively-licensed public medical models — all are either incomplete, demo-stage, or narrow to one condition/pipeline. PARTIAL.

### P7: Low-cost, phone-camera-only AI accessibility tools as an alternative to expensive dedicated hardware
- **What it is**: Using a standard smartphone camera plus a vision-language model to deliver navigation/object-recognition assistance that otherwise requires a ~$2,000 dedicated AI wearable.
- **Evidence**: Envision Glasses (AI smart glasses for blind/low-vision users) cost nearly $2,000 for the most basic model, cited as a user-facing cost barrier (via WebSearch "AI accessibility assistive technology blind low vision open source"). The same search surfaced an open-source "AI-Powered Assistive Navigation System" on GitHub (MIT license) adding spatial memory and 3D audio guidance, built because its author was frustrated by "the statelessness of most consumer assistive vision tools."
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: The open-source alternative is an early-stage GitHub project with no independent review or benchmarking against the commercial hardware it's positioned against; a related paper on Meta Ray-Ban-based accessibility apps separately notes "none of the presented applications have reached BVI users" despite the underlying tech being available.
- **Maturity**: SATURATED
- **Verdict notes**: Verified Be My AI (inside the free Be My Eyes app) already ships exactly this: phone-camera-only AI image description/navigation help, at massive scale (1M+ blind/low-vision users, 10M+ volunteers as of March 2026 per company reporting), free, backed by a dedicated foundation to keep it free. Microsoft's Seeing AI is a comparable second shipped free product in the same space. Open-source alternatives (VISTA, MIT-licensed; Gator) and a Harvard research app (Mobilio, reported faster navigation/fewer collisions than Google Maps+cane in trials) further confirm this gap is being actively and successfully closed by multiple well-resourced and community efforts, not just discussed.

- **Evidence**: A design guide found via WebSearch ("applied AI underserved communities subreddit reddit nonprofit accessibility low-resource languages") explicitly recommends "voice prompts and pictorial icons for low-literacy users" instead of "text-heavy budget dashboards," noting many underserved areas have "sporadic or no internet connectivity."
- **Why it looks unbuilt/untested**: Framed in the source as a design recommendation/guideline rather than a shipped, tested product — no named implementation surfaced in the same search to point to as already solving this.
- **Maturity**: PARTIAL

But the shipped implementation is confined to one vertical (banking), with no public technical case study or reusable toolkit/component library for other underserved-domain AI products to adopt the same pattern.
