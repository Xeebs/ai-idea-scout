# AI ASL Interpreting Vendor Co-Governance Checklist

A self-contained prompt template for anyone evaluating a specific AI ASL (American Sign Language) interpreting vendor — a school district accessibility lead, a hospital interpreter-services manager, a court administrator, or anyone else deciding whether to adopt one of these tools — who needs a fast, defensible read on whether the vendor's "Deaf-led" or "advisory committee" language reflects real decision-making power or is just marketing. Paste this whole template plus your inputs into any AI chat tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, etc.).

## Why this exists
Several companies (Nagish, Sorenson, Silence Speaks, Signapse, DeepMind's SL2T, and others) now pitch real-time AI ASL interpreting as a solution to the rural/low-access interpreter shortage. But a global survey of Deaf/Hard-of-Hearing community members on sign-language technologies found the top two concerns were "hearing people making money from sign language technologies" and "few Deaf people leading or contributing." Vendors often publish an "AI Sign Language Advisory Committee" or similar language without saying whether that body actually has authority to block a product decision, whether its members are compensated, or whether it's majority-Deaf at all. This checklist exists to separate specific, checkable claims from vague reassurance — one vendor at a time, for one procurement decision.

This is the fast, one-shot checklist version of a larger idea for a standing, independently Deaf-reviewed public registry across all major vendors. This template does not replace that registry — it can't independently verify anything, it only checks how specific a vendor's own published claims are.

## What you need to provide
**Data source: Manual.** This only works from text you paste or link yourself — no tool can pull a company's internal governance structure from anywhere, because it isn't the kind of thing any API or connector exposes. You need:

1. **The vendor's name.**
2. **At least one piece of the vendor's own published material about governance, an advisory board, or Deaf involvement** — a quote, a URL's text, a press release, or their written RFP answer. Paste the actual text, not your summary of it.

Optional, if you have it:

3. **Your own notes from a sales call or vendor Q&A** — direct quotes work best.
4. **Any third-party evidence** you want weighed in: Deaf community commentary (e.g. r/ASLinterpreters, NAD or RID statements), the EUD's 15-principle ethical framework, or the Cambridge/Minderoo procurement report.

If all you have is generic marketing copy with no names, numbers, or described process, that's fine — say so, and the checklist will score every criterion accordingly rather than guessing at detail you didn't provide.

## What it checks
Using only the material you paste, this scores the vendor against five specific criteria, each as **PASS**, **PARTIAL**, **CLAIMS-ONLY**, or **NO-EVIDENCE**:

1. **Majority-Deaf authority** — does the governing/advisory body state it is majority-Deaf by actual roster or ratio, not just "includes Deaf members"?
2. **Published decision-making power** — can the body block or require changes to a product decision, or does it only "provide input"?
3. **Responsiveness track record** — is there a specific, dated example of the vendor changing something because of Deaf-reviewer feedback?
4. **Reviewer compensation** — are Deaf advisors/reviewers paid, and how? (Uncompensated "co-creation" risks tokenism even when well-intentioned — this is a known finding in sign-language-AI co-design research, not a cynical assumption.)
5. **Data/signer representation** — does the vendor say anything specific about how many signers, or which regions/dialects, its data represents?

For each criterion scored below PASS, you get one sentence naming exactly what document or detail would move it to PASS — so you know what to ask the vendor for next, not just that something's missing.

## What you get back
A table scoring all five criteria with the exact quote or "not addressed" behind each score, a short list of what would close each gap, and a 2-4 sentence overall read on whether the vendor's claims, as given, support "co-governance" or read mostly as self-reported marketing language.

**What this is not**: an audit, a verification of whether a stated committee actually functions as claimed, or a recommendation to sign or not sign. It only checks how specific the vendor's own claims are. A PASS means "the claim is specific and checkable," not "Deaf reviewers have confirmed it's true." Procurement risk, cost, and timeline are still your call.

## Example of how to use it
Paste this template, then add:
- Vendor: [name]
- Material: [paste the governance/advisory page text, press release, or RFP answer verbatim]
- Sales call notes (optional): [paste any direct quotes]
- Third-party evidence (optional): [paste any community commentary or framework excerpt you want weighed in]

The assistant will return the five-criterion table, the gap-closing list, and the overall read — nothing more, nothing less.
