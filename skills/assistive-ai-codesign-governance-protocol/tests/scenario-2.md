# Scenario 2: Mobility-tech startup with real but incomplete co-design

## Input

**Product and community**: "PathPilot" is an AI-powered indoor wayfinding app for wheelchair users, giving real-time route guidance that avoids inaccessible paths (stairs, narrow doors, broken curb cuts) in large public buildings. We're preparing to apply for the cross-disability co-design certification described in our organization's protocol.

**Engagement process so far**: "We partnered with the Metro Wheelchair Users Alliance (a real local advocacy nonprofit with about 200 members) starting at the problem-definition stage, eight months before launch. We ran three paid co-design workshops ($75/session per participant, 12 different members across the three sessions, deliberately including both manual and power wheelchair users plus two members who also have low vision) where they mapped out what 'inaccessible path' should even mean and reviewed our routing logic. We have a public changelog page listing specific features that came from those sessions (e.g., 'added narrow-doorway width threshold per workshop feedback') versus ones we decided ourselves (e.g., 'offline map caching was our call, not raised by partners'). However, final launch decisions are still made solely by our product lead; the Alliance reviews and comments but doesn't have a veto. We haven't done any further sessions since the third workshop two months ago and don't have one scheduled."

## Why this is realistic
This represents a team doing meaningfully more than token consultation — named partner, compensation, design-stage timing, transparency artifact, and real representation breadth — while still falling short on two specific, named gaps (no veto power, no continuity plan), which is the common realistic shape for a team that is close to certification-ready but not quite there.

## Expected output characteristics (pass/fail checklist)

- [ ] **Criterion 1 (named partner)**: MET — "Metro Wheelchair Users Alliance" is a specific named organization.
- [ ] **Criterion 2 (compensation)**: MET — $75/session stated explicitly.
- [ ] **Criterion 3 (timing)**: MET — explicitly "problem-definition stage, eight months before launch."
- [ ] **Criterion 4 (veto power)**: MISSING or PARTIAL — input explicitly states the Alliance "doesn't have a veto"; must not be marked MET.
- [ ] **Criterion 5 (transparency artifact)**: MET — public changelog distinguishing community-driven vs. unilateral decisions is described concretely.
- [ ] **Criterion 6 (representation breadth)**: MET — 12 members across sessions with stated diversity (manual/power chair users, two with low vision also).
- [ ] **Criterion 7 (continuity)**: MISSING or PARTIAL — no further sessions since two months ago and none scheduled; must be flagged, not ignored.
- [ ] **Hard-fail check**: NOT triggered (criterion 3 is MET) — report must say so explicitly rather than treating the veto-power gap as a hard fail (it isn't one; only criterion 3 triggers the hard fail per the rubric).
- [ ] **Readiness tier**: Needs Evidence (5 MET, 2 gaps with a plausible path to closing them) — must not be marked Certification-Ready given two unmet criteria, and must not be marked Not Ready given the strength of the other five.
- [ ] **Priority actions**: specific to the two real gaps — e.g., "define and document a concrete veto mechanism for the Alliance over launch decisions" and "schedule a continuity check-in or ongoing review cadence with the Alliance before applying" — not generic filler, and not inventing additional gaps that weren't in the input.
- [ ] **Limits disclaimer present**.

## Fail conditions
- Marking criterion 4 MET despite the input explicitly denying veto power.
- Triggering the hard-fail condition when criterion 3 was clearly MET.
- Rating the overall tier as Certification-Ready or as Not Ready instead of Needs Evidence.
