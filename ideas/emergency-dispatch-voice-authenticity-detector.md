# Dispatch Shield: Real-Time Voice-Clone Detection Built for 911 Dispatch Workflows

- **Status**: PROPOSED
- **Date**: 2026-11-13
- **Topic**: Voice & multimodal agents
- **Source**: research/2026-11-13-voice-multimodal-agents.md · P7
- **Maturity verdict**: PARTIAL (shipped products exist for the adjacent general problem — Resemble AI's "Detect" and Reality Defender's real-time API are both sold into contact centers for fraud/authentication use cases, and Resemble's own blog claims, unverified and with states unnamed, that "some states have started testing AI voice filters for 911 call centers"; dispatch-software vendor Intertalk Systems has only run a webinar pairing emotion-recognition with voice-deepfake-detection, not a shipping integration. No evidence found of broadly adopted, dispatcher-specific — as opposed to generic contact-center — verification tooling actually deployed at 911 call centers.)
- **Format**: PROJECT
- **Data Sources**: CONNECTED — ingests the live call audio stream from a dispatch center's existing SIPREC-based call-recording tap (standard, mandated call-logging infrastructure already deployed at most modern PSAPs) and runs it through a real-time voice-liveness/synthesis-detection model. No audio is pasted or uploaded manually; the SIPREC integration point is mature and already present, the detection-and-dispatcher-workflow layer is the new piece this project builds.

## The Gap
Hacker News's front-page story "Swatters are using AI voices to deceive 911 dispatchers, and it's getting worse" (npr.org via HN, 2026-11-11, 12 points, 4 comments) states the problem explicitly in its own headline: it is getting worse, meaning no adopted dispatcher-side detection or verification tooling is keeping pace with voice-cloning capability. Generic voice-authenticity products (Resemble Detect, Reality Defender) exist, but they're built and sold for contact-center fraud/authentication, not integrated into a 911 dispatcher's actual CAD workflow or tuned against the specific false-positive risk profile of emergency calls (panic, poor cell reception, background noise, speech disorders).

## The Project
A real-time voice-authenticity monitoring layer purpose-built for 911 dispatch, not repurposed contact-center fraud software. It taps the call audio via the dispatch center's existing SIPREC call-recording infrastructure (no new phone hardware required), runs a continuously-scoring speech-synthesis/voice-liveness detector on the live stream, and surfaces a plain-language risk indicator on the dispatcher's CAD screen within the first 10-15 seconds of the call — deliberately calibrated to avoid false-alarm fatigue, since this environment's cost of a false positive (delaying or biasing response to a real emergency) is far higher than a contact center's. Every flagged call is logged with audio evidence and model confidence for supervisor review; the system never auto-deprioritizes a call, it only informs the human decision.

Components: a SIPREC audio-stream tap, a real-time anti-spoofing/liveness scoring model tuned against both synthetic-voice samples and benign edge cases (voice changers, bad connections, accented or disordered speech), a dispatcher-facing CAD-screen flag UI, a flagged-call audit log, and a dispatcher training/runbook companion explaining what a flag means and what action it does and doesn't justify.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: the roughly 5,300 Public Safety Answering Points across the US — the FCC's 17th Annual 911 Fee Report counts 4,693 primary plus 635 secondary PSAPs for 2024, together handling over 213 million reported voice 911 calls that year — specifically the dispatchers staffing them, and by extension the households targeted by AI-voice-enabled swatting, where each false SWAT deployment carries real risk of violent escalation against innocent occupants.
**Mechanism**: today a dispatcher has no tooling to distinguish a cloned voice from a genuine caller in real time, so an AI-generated swatting call gets the same urgency and trust as a real emergency, triggering an armed response to an address with no actual threat. A dispatcher-facing authenticity flag, validated against a tuned confidence threshold and logged for review, gives a dispatch supervisor a documented second signal to factor into response posture (e.g., prioritizing officer verification-by-callback) without delaying or downgrading any call that scores as likely-genuine.
**Measure**: detection precision/recall against a labeled corpus of known synthetic-voice calls versus benign edge cases, with the false-positive rate on legitimate emergencies tracked as the primary gate (target near zero before any deployment); number of flagged calls that independently correlate with a later-confirmed swatting incident; time from call start to flag surfaced on the dispatcher's screen (target: well before a unit would otherwise be dispatched).

## Why Now
Commodity real-time voice-liveness/anti-spoofing models — the same class powering Resemble Detect and Reality Defender's contact-center products — are now fast and cheap enough to run per-call at the latency a live emergency call demands, and SIPREC-based call-recording taps are already standard, mandated infrastructure at most PSAPs, meaning the integration point already exists. The missing piece is a detection layer and dispatcher workflow purpose-built for the swatting use case rather than generic financial-fraud authentication — exactly the niche the NPR/HN story shows nobody has filled yet.

## MVP Sketch
Partner with one PSAP or a dispatch-software vendor's sandbox environment to pull a SIPREC feed into a prototype built on an existing open or licensed anti-spoofing model (e.g., an ASVspoof-derived detector), tuned against a small labeled test set: synthetic-voice samples from current cloning tools plus a benign-edge-case set (voice-changer apps, poor-connection audio, accented/disordered speech). Single experiment: can the detector hit a false-positive rate low enough to be dispatcher-usable at all (e.g., under 1% on the benign-edge-case set) while still catching a meaningful fraction of synthetic-voice test calls — if not, the idea is killed at the prototype stage before any CAD integration work begins.

## Risks & Open Questions
- False positives are the central risk: flagging a real emergency caller as "possibly synthetic" due to a bad connection, a speech disorder, or an accent could delay or bias a response — the design must bias hard toward "log and inform," never toward auto-deprioritizing a call.
- No public benchmark exists for synthetic-voice detection on real-world 911-call audio conditions (panic, background noise, poor cell reception); building a credible labeled test set likely requires months of work with PSAP or law-enforcement partners before the detector can be honestly validated.
- Resemble AI's own blog claim that "some states have started testing AI voice filters for 911 call centers" is unverified but could mean this gap closes faster than expected if a major vendor ships a dispatcher-specific product first; differentiation would need to be openness/auditability or an affordable tier for smaller/rural PSAPs priced out of enterprise contact-center-fraud contracts.
- Integrating into live CAD/dispatch software is a regulated, safety-critical government procurement process (state/county purchasing, NG911 standards compliance) that moves far slower than a typical sales cycle — pilot-to-deployment timelines should be expected in years, not months, regardless of how ready the underlying detection tech is.
