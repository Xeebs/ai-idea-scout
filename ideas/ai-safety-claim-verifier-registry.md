# Ledger of Trust: An Independent Conflict-of-Interest and Calibration Registry for AI-Safety Claim Verifiers

- **Status**: PROPOSED
- **Date**: 2026-11-18
- **Topic**: AI safety & guardrails in production
- **Source**: research/2026-11-18-ai-safety-guardrails-in-production.md · P8
- **Maturity verdict**: PARTIAL — real but acknowledged-inadequate attempts exist: METR performs independent reviews of AI developers' risk assessments but its own independence is contested (Anthropic's Dario Amodei publicly defending it despite close engagement); AVERI, a new nonprofit founded by an ex-OpenAI policy lead, was created to address this exact gap but is too recent/unproven to count as closing it; a 2026 "Frontier AI Auditing" paper (arXiv 2601.11699) states current third-party AI assessments lack features standard in other audited industries and that almost no frontier lab submits to binding independent external audit. Institutions exist but are contested, unproven, or non-binding — not a closed gap.
- **Format**: BOTH
- **Data Sources**: CONNECTED (project) — pulls nonprofit tax filings via ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer API (mature, free, already indexes 990 filings for US nonprofits) and incident cross-references via the AI Incident Database's API (aiid.org, mature, free) to build the funding-ledger and calibration-tracker components; press-release and partnership-announcement research is editorial/manual curation layered on top, since no API aggregates "which safety org has a financial tie to which lab" directly — the registry is honestly a hybrid of connected data pulls and human-curated sourcing, not a fully automated pipeline. MANUAL (skill) — the user pastes the specific claim text, the name of the organization or individual making it, and any funding/partnership language they've already found.

## The Gap
Two concurrent Hacker News items from this digest's window: "How 'Independent' AI Safety Groups Are Pumping Anthropic IPO" questions whether ostensibly independent safety evaluators are effectively promotional ahead of a lab's IPO, and "Anthropic says a key AI safety alarm has been triggered, days before its IPO" raises the same credibility question from the other direction — a lab's own internal alarm, timed just before a high-stakes corporate event. Neither item, nor anything else found in a genuine multi-channel search, points to a neutral, sourced mechanism for checking whether a given safety claim's author has an undisclosed financial or structural tie to the lab being evaluated, or whether that author's past claims actually held up.

## The Project
A continuously maintained, publicly browsable registry profiling every organization that issues or is cited as issuing an "independent" AI-safety assessment of a frontier lab — METR, Apollo Research, UK AISI, US CAISI, AVERI, and the handful of academic audit groups active today. Three linked components per profile: (1) a funding and affiliation ledger, sourced from ProPublica-indexed nonprofit tax filings, press releases, and any self-disclosed conflict-of-interest statements, tagging each evaluator-lab pair with the type and scale of financial or structural tie found (grants, compute credits, consulting fees, board overlaps, co-founder history); (2) a timeline correlating each public safety statement with concurrent corporate events at the evaluated lab (funding rounds, IPO filings, major launches), flagging same-week or same-month proximity as a disclosed-but-not-adjudicated signal, never an accusation; (3) a retrospective calibration tracker checking whether a past risk claim or clearance was later contradicted by a real incident, cross-referenced against the AI Incident Database. Every entry links its primary sources so a reader can verify the underlying claim themselves instead of trusting the registry's framing.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: journalists, congressional and EU AI Office policy staffers, and enterprise AI-vendor-risk or compliance teams who today manually dig through tax filings, press archives, and scattered disclosures any time a lab or an "independent" evaluator makes a public safety claim worth citing in an article, hearing, or vendor-risk memo. The named evaluator roster is small today — well under 20 organizations, per the Frontier AI Auditing paper's own finding that almost no lab submits to binding external audit — multiplied by the dozens of outlets and committee staffers who cite one of them whenever a claim like the Anthropic IPO-timed alarm goes public.
**Mechanism**: checking whether a safety claim's source has an undisclosed financial or structural tie to the lab it evaluates currently requires piecing together tax filings, press releases, and board listings by hand, under deadline pressure — which means most citations happen without that check ever being done. A single, sourced, continuously maintained registry turns that check into a lookup instead of a research project, raising the cost of an unexamined claim being repeated uncritically and lowering the cost of catching one that warrants real scrutiny.
**Measure**: number of previously undisclosed-but-publicly-discoverable funding or affiliation ties surfaced per evaluator-lab pair profiled, a direct signal the registry is doing real work rather than restating known facts; time from a new safety claim being published to a sourced dossier existing for its author (target: under 48 hours, versus the days-to-weeks a one-off investigative piece takes today); citations of the registry's entries in subsequent news coverage, policy documents, or vendor-risk reviews, tracked via backlink/citation monitoring, as a proxy for whether it displaces the manual-research status quo.

## Why Now
The Anthropic IPO-timed safety-alarm story and the "pumping the IPO" HN critique both landed in this same 30-day window, showing live public appetite for exactly this check right now. AVERI's founding by an ex-OpenAI policy lead confirms insiders recognize the same gap — but a brand-new, single-org nonprofit is itself an interested party whose own independence needs the same scrutiny, which is precisely why a registry, not another single evaluator, is the right shape for this gap. ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer API and the AI Incident Database are both mature, free, queryable data sources today, making the automatable half of this buildable immediately rather than waiting on new infrastructure.

## Skill Angle
Trigger: someone encounters a new public AI-safety claim or evaluator endorsement — a press release, an HN post, a quoted expert in a news article — and wants a quick first-pass conflict-of-interest read before citing or amplifying it. **Data source**: Manual — the user pastes the claim text, the name of the organization or individual making it, and any funding/partnership language they've already turned up. The skill checks whether the org already has a registry profile or, if not, drafts a starter dossier by pulling the org's 990 filing via ProPublica, logging recent press mentions of partnerships with the evaluated lab, and noting any self-disclosed conflict-of-interest statement, then returns known ties, timing proximity to the lab's concurrent corporate events, and a confidence rating on how complete the picture is. The project is the always-growing, sourced registry; the skill is the one-shot first-pass check someone runs before deciding a claim needs deeper scrutiny.

## MVP Sketch
Hand-build profiles for the five or six organizations named in this cycle's evidence (METR, AVERI, UK AISI, Apollo Research, plus Anthropic and OpenAI as the labs being evaluated): pull each nonprofit's 990 via ProPublica's API, log disclosed grants or partnerships from press archives, and timeline their public statements against the labs' own press-release calendars. Ship as a static, fully sourced web page first, not a live-updating service, plus a one-shot skill that repeats the same research steps for a new org/claim pair. Single experiment: profile METR specifically and time how long it takes to trace its funding disclosures and the Dario-Amodei-defends-independence episode to primary sources, compared with how long that apparently took the HN poster who raised the original complaint. 2-3 weeks, 1-2 people.

## Risks & Open Questions
- This sits closer to investigative journalism than software; the real bottleneck may be unstructured primary-source research (press archives, informal partnership announcements) that resists full automation more than clean API ingestion — the MVP should prove how much can realistically stay automated before promising a live-updating registry.
- A registry that flags timing proximity without adjudicating causation risks being read as an accusation regardless of disclaimers; it needs a transparent, sourced-only, no-verdict editorial policy to stay defensible and avoid looking like a hit piece itself.
- AVERI or a similarly well-funded new entrant could build exactly this as part of its own mandate, which would be a reasonable outcome for closing the gap but would undercut a standalone project's differentiation — worth tracking as a built-elsewhere risk, not a reason to wait.
- Community-demand signal this cycle is two Hacker News items at low-to-moderate engagement (3 points and 15 points/12 comments), not a groundswell — real validation is whether journalists or policy staff actually adopt a lookup tool, not whether people upvote the underlying complaint.
