# Threat Tune: A Self-Service Abuse-Classifier Tuning Toolkit for Platforms Below Reddit's Scale

- **Status**: PROPOSED
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Topic**: AI safety & guardrails in production
- **Source**: research/2026-11-19-ai-safety-guardrails-in-production.md · P8
- **Maturity verdict**: PARTIAL — Reddit's own engineering writeup reports a bespoke centralized guardrail (F1 0.97, sub-25ms p99) built because "existing foundation-model guardrails failed to address Reddit's unique threat surface." WebSearch found the low-latency serving half of this now accessible as open-source (Bifrost at ~11μs overhead/5,000 RPS, Portkey's 50+-guardrail gateway), but neither ships a toolkit for training/tuning a classifier to a platform's own specific abuse patterns the way Reddit's F1-0.97 model does — that half remains unproductized, and no GitHub/product search surfaced a self-service version of it.
- **Format**: BOTH
- **Data Sources**: CONNECTED — reads a platform's own existing moderation action log (the system of record for human-moderator decisions: content, the action taken, reviewer, and timestamp) — e.g., a trust-and-safety case-management tool's export API, or a direct read of the raw moderation-queue database/table most platforms already maintain. No universal connector exists since every platform's mod-queue schema differs, so a thin per-platform adapter (a mapping config plus a CSV/JSON/DB-row import) is part of this build, not a from-scratch data-collection project. Low-latency serving reuses the already-mature OSS gateways (Bifrost, Portkey) rather than building a new one.

## The Gap
Reddit's writeup is a single large platform's bespoke internal build, not something a smaller team can adopt — the digest's own verdict names the gap precisely as "the absence of an accessible, reusable version of this pattern... for teams below Reddit's scale." The low-latency gateway piece of that pattern has since become open-source and accessible (Bifrost, Portkey), but the piece Reddit's post actually highlights as the hard part — tuning a classifier to the platform's *own* specific abuse patterns rather than generic foundation-model categories — has no self-service equivalent. A mid-size forum, marketplace, dating app, or gaming platform today either accepts a generic guardrail's FP/FN rate tuned to nobody's actual traffic, or would need to replicate Reddit's from-scratch ML effort itself.

## The Project
A self-service pipeline that turns a platform's own moderation action log into a tuned, low-latency classifier without requiring an in-house ML team. The toolkit imports a sample of a platform's human-moderator decisions (content plus action taken), runs an active-learning loop that surfaces the most uncertain/informative cases for a reviewer to confirm or correct, fine-tunes a small distilled classifier on the resulting labeled set, and deploys it behind an already-mature low-latency OSS gateway (Bifrost or Portkey) in front of (not replacing) the platform's existing foundation-model guardrail. The centerpiece is the labeling-to-tuned-classifier loop built for a platform's *own* abuse taxonomy — gaming trash-talk, dating-app harassment euphemisms, marketplace scam phrasing, or whatever a given platform's moderation log actually contains — not a pre-trained model for any single vocabulary domain.

Components: a mod-log import adapter (per-platform mapping config); an active-learning sampling loop over unlabeled recent traffic; a small distilled-classifier fine-tuning step; a benchmark report comparing the tuned classifier against the platform's existing generic guardrail on a held-out slice of its own mod queue; a deployment wiring into Bifrost/Portkey.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: trust-and-safety and engineering teams at mid-size platforms with real but non-Reddit-scale moderation volume (forums, marketplaces, dating apps, gaming communities, SaaS products with user-generated content) who maintain the combined ~18k-GitHub-star NeMo Guardrails/Guardrails AI/LLM Guard ecosystem's generic checks today but have no engineering budget to replicate a bespoke F1-0.97 build — a materially broader and more varied population than teams whose false-positive problem is specifically technical/developer vocabulary (the narrower gap a prior idea in this ledger, `technical-content-guardrail-false-positive-reducer.md`, already targets).
**Mechanism**: today a platform's guardrail misfires are measured, if at all, only after a complaint or incident, because nobody can afford the ML investment Reddit made to characterize its *own* traffic; a self-service pipeline that starts from the platform's own already-labeled moderation history (every past human mod decision is a free label) collapses that investment into pointing the tool at existing logs and reviewing an active-learning sample, turning a multi-month bespoke ML build into a days-long tuning run.
**Measure**: F1/false-positive-rate of the tuned classifier vs. the platform's prior generic guardrail, measured on the platform's own held-out moderation-queue sample; time from "point the tool at our mod log" to "a measured, deployed tuned classifier" (target: days, not Reddit's in-house multi-month build); added p99 latency vs. the generic-guardrail baseline (target: comparable to Bifrost/Portkey's existing sub-millisecond overhead).

## Why Now
Reddit's writeup (this digest's direct evidence) is a recent, numbers-backed proof the gap is real and costly to close from scratch; the low-latency serving layer this toolkit depends on (Bifrost, Portkey) only became a mature, accessible open-source option this year, and active-learning fine-tuning of small distilled classifiers is now commodity-cheap — the missing piece is exactly the self-service labeling-to-tuning pipeline, not any underlying model capability.

## Skill Angle
Trigger: a team hands over a sample export of their existing moderation log (content, human-mod action, timestamp) and a description of their current guardrail's known false positives/negatives. The skill estimates, from that sample, roughly how much FP/FN improvement a tuned classifier would likely recover and whether the sample size and label consistency are sufficient to justify the active-learning build — a one-shot "is this worth doing" diagnostic before committing to the always-running training/deployment pipeline. The project is the actual training loop and deployed classifier; the skill is the feasibility read that precedes it.

## MVP Sketch
A CLI tool that ingests a CSV/JSON export of 500–1,000 recent moderation decisions, runs a simple uncertainty-sampling active-learning loop against a held-out slice to pick the next 50–100 cases for a reviewer to confirm, fine-tunes a small open classifier (DistilBERT-class) on the resulting labeled set, and benchmarks it against the platform's stated current guardrail config on a separate held-out slice. Single experiment: using one willing mid-size platform's real (anonymized) mod log, show the tuned classifier's F1 on their own held-out traffic beats their generic guardrail's measured F1 on the same traffic. 3–4 weeks, 1–2 people.

## Risks & Open Questions
- A platform's moderation log may be too small, inconsistent, or policy-drifted to produce a reliable tuned classifier — the MVP's real test is whether the active-learning loop can work with realistically modest label volumes, not an idealized large dataset.
- A foundation-model vendor could ship a native "bring your own mod log to fine-tune our guardrail" feature directly, which would commoditize this faster than a standalone toolkit can differentiate.
- Overlaps conceptually with `technical-content-guardrail-false-positive-reducer.md` in citing the same Reddit evidence, but targets a materially broader population (any platform's own abuse taxonomy) with a different mechanism (a generic self-service active-learning pipeline vs. a pre-built classifier for one vocabulary domain) — worth re-checking for actual overlap once both have pilot users.
- Getting a platform to hand over real moderation-log data, even anonymized, is itself a trust hurdle that may slow early adoption more than the technical build does.
