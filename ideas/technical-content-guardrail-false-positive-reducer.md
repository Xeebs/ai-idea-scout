# Code-Safe: A Reusable False-Positive-Reduction Layer for Guardrails on Technical Content

- **Status**: EXPLORING
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Topic**: AI safety & guardrails in production
- **Source**: research/2026-11-12-ai-safety-guardrails-production.md · P8
- **Maturity verdict**: PARTIAL — WebSearch found the problem well documented (a guardrails survey noting code-related queries are frequently misclassified as exploits; tianpan.co's "Alignment Tax" piece with concrete examples like "kill the background process" getting flagged; Zilliz/Tetrate docs with generic mitigation advice — tiered checks, custom labeled eval sets) but no named off-the-shelf product or open-source classifier specifically tuned to cut false positives on technical/developer content. Reddit built and published results on its own in-house service (F1 0.97, sub-25ms p99) rather than adopting one — advice exists, a reusable solution does not.
- **Format**: BOTH
- **Data Sources**: MANUAL — the team pastes a sample of queries their current guardrail has false-positived on (the query text plus the guardrail's verdict and, ideally, a human-confirmed ground-truth label), used to build and evaluate a technical-content eval set and tune the classifier. A CONNECTED upgrade — pulling flagged-but-benign query logs directly from the team's existing guardrail gateway (NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, LLM Guard, or a commercial gateway's audit log/webhook) instead of a manual export — is the natural v2, since most of these already log every block decision; no purpose-built connector exists today, so MANUAL is the honest classification for the MVP.

## The Gap
A Reddit engineering write-up describes building its own in-house guardrails platform because "existing foundation-model guardrails produced too many false positives on technical queries," reporting an F1 of 0.97 at sub-25ms p99 latency after the custom build. The pattern recurs in the research (Tian Pan's "Alignment Tax" examples like a sysadmin's "kill the background process" getting flagged as violent intent) but every mitigation found was generic advice (tiered checks, custom labeled eval sets) rather than a reusable classifier or library a second team could adopt — meaning any company building a developer-tool, security-tooling, or DevOps-facing AI product with today's off-the-shelf guardrails is likely re-deriving Reddit's build from scratch.

## The Project
An open, fine-tunable classification layer (a small distilled model plus a rules pre-filter) purpose-built to sit in front of a general foundation-model guardrail specifically for technical/developer content, trained and evaluated on a purpose-built labeled corpus of benign-but-alarming-sounding technical phrases (process-kill commands, exploit/CVE discussion in a security-research context, "attack," "payload," "inject," "bypass" used in their mundane engineering sense) paired with genuinely malicious uses of the same vocabulary. The centerpiece is the labeled eval set and the measured false-positive-reduction delta it produces when inserted ahead of a stock guardrail (NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, LLM Guard, or a vendor gateway), packaged as a drop-in pre-filter rather than a wholesale guardrail replacement, so teams keep their existing safety coverage for genuinely malicious intent while recovering legitimate technical traffic that generic guardrails currently block.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: engineering teams at companies building AI coding assistants, DevOps/SRE copilots, and security-research or pentest-support tools — products whose core legitimate traffic is dense with vocabulary ("kill," "exploit," "payload," "inject," "bypass") that generic safety classifiers associate with violence or malicious intent. GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Replit Agent, and the dozens of smaller AI-coding and AI-security-tooling startups cataloged on product directories like There's An AI For That's "developer tools" category are a reasonable proxy for the population size — likely hundreds of teams shipping guardrail-wrapped technical assistants today.
**Mechanism**: a generic foundation-model guardrail trained on broad internet toxicity/safety data over-indexes on surface vocabulary, blocking legitimate technical requests ("write a script to kill this process") at a rate high enough that Reddit's own engineers judged it worth building a custom F1-0.97 replacement rather than tolerating it; a reusable pre-filter trained specifically on technical-benign-vs-malicious pairs lets smaller teams without Reddit's engineering budget recover that lost legitimate traffic without weakening coverage against actual exploit requests.
**Measure**: false-positive rate on a held-out technical-benign query set, before vs. after inserting the pre-filter ahead of a stock guardrail (target: approach Reddit's reported 0.97 F1 without a bespoke build); missed-malicious-intent rate on the same corpus's genuinely harmful pairs (must not regress); added p99 latency from the pre-filter stage (target: comparable to Reddit's reported sub-25ms).

## Why Now
Reddit's write-up (surfaced in this digest's window) is a concrete, recent, numbers-backed proof that this specific gap costs engineering time worth building around; the underlying technique (a small distilled classifier fine-tuned on a targeted labeled corpus, run ahead of a general guardrail) requires no new model capability, just assembling a dataset and training pipeline nobody has published as a reusable artifact yet.

## Skill Angle
Trigger: a team hands over a sample of queries their current guardrail incorrectly blocked, with the intended (benign) interpretation noted. The skill reviews the sample, drafts a starter labeled eval set in the technical-benign-vs-malicious pair format, and recommends which generic guardrail categories are the likely source of the false positives (e.g., "violence" category triggering on process-management vocabulary) as a one-shot diagnostic. The project is the trained pre-filter model and the maintained, growing eval corpus that a team actually deploys in front of their production guardrail and keeps running — the skill is the design/diagnosis step that precedes committing to training and deploying it.

## MVP Sketch
Hand-label roughly 200–300 technical-benign queries (scraped/adapted from Stack Overflow, security-research mailing lists, and devops runbooks) paired with a matched set of genuinely malicious queries using the same vocabulary, fine-tune a small open classifier (e.g., a DistilBERT-class model) on the pairs, and insert it as a pre-filter ahead of a stock NeMo Guardrails or Guardrails AI pipeline. Single experiment: measure false-positive rate on a held-out technical-benign split with vs. without the pre-filter, and confirm missed-malicious rate on the matched malicious split doesn't regress.

## Risks & Open Questions
- Labeling "benign vs. malicious" technical intent is itself genuinely ambiguous at the margins (a real exploit walkthrough and a security-research explanation can read identically) — the eval set's label quality is the whole product and needs domain-expert review, not just crowd labeling.
- A foundation-model vendor (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) could close this gap directly by fine-tuning their own safety classifiers on technical corpora, which would commoditize the pre-filter faster than a small team's eval set can stay differentiated.
- Reddit's 0.97 F1 is self-reported with no published methodology or held-out set shared publicly — the MVP's real validation bar is building an independently credible eval set, not matching an unverifiable number.
- This targets a narrower, already-technical audience than most guardrail ideas in this cycle; the population estimate (hundreds of teams) is a rough proxy, not a verified count, and should be revisited once a few pilot teams are identified.
