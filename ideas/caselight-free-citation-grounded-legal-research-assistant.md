## The Gap
Hacker News Show HN "CaseCite – Free RAG caselaw research and document analysis for lawyers" (github.com/thomasunise/casecite, 2026-10-25, 14 points / 2 comments) names a real gap: in a professional vertical (legal) where most commercial RAG/legal-tech offerings are paid and closed, a free, citation-first vertical RAG tool has modest but real community interest. The gap-analyst's search confirmed this isn't imaginary — Harvard LIL, Free Law Project, and a Colorado-focused project are all independently working pieces of the same problem — but none of them has shipped a single, maintained, consolidated tool that a non-lawyer could actually use for real case research today.

## The Project
CaseLight consolidates the fragmented free/open pieces the gap-analyst found into one maintained, free, citation-grounded legal research assistant purpose-built for people who cannot afford Westlaw or Lexis: self-represented ("pro se") litigants and the legal aid clinics that serve them. The novel pattern is the centerpiece: rather than classic open-ended RAG, every generated claim must trace to a directly quoted span from a case retrieved live from CourtListener — if the system can't find a quote supporting a claim, it says so instead of generating one, closing off the single most damaging legal-RAG failure mode (a hallucinated or non-existent citation, which has already caused sanctioned filings in several widely reported real cases). Components: (1) a CourtListener/RECAP retrieval layer, (2) Eyecite for citation parsing and normalization, (3) a strict quote-or-refuse generation check, (4) a plain-language explanation layer that translates legal jargon for a non-lawyer reader, with every claim linked back to the exact CourtListener source passage.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: Self-represented litigants in U.S. civil courts — commonly cited National Center for State Courts research shows unrepresented parties make up the majority of litigants in high-volume case types like eviction and debt collection (frequently reported in the 70-90% range depending on jurisdiction and case type) — plus the roughly 130 Legal Services Corporation-funded legal aid programs nationally, which by LSC's own reporting can serve only a fraction of income-eligible clients due to capacity constraints and limited paid-database seats.
**Mechanism**: Gives pro se litigants and capacity-constrained legal aid staff a free, citation-verified starting point for case research that today requires either a paid Westlaw/Lexis seat (which clinics ration due to cost) or hours of unguided manual search across public but hard-to-navigate caselaw databases. The quote-or-refuse check specifically targets the known RAG failure mode — a fabricated citation — that makes pointing a pro se litigant at a general-purpose chatbot risky rather than helpful, making CaseLight materially safer for this specific, high-stakes, low-expertise audience than an unconstrained alternative.
**Measure**: (1) citation hallucination rate on a held-out eval set, verified against CourtListener's own database (target: 0% non-existent citations); (2) time-to-first-relevant-case for a sampled set of common pro se scenarios (eviction defense, small claims, protective orders) versus a manual-search baseline; (3) pilot adoption and self-reported researcher-time saved per case among partnering legal aid clinics.

## Why Now
CourtListener/RECAP has matured into a comprehensive, free, actively maintained public API covering the bulk of U.S. federal and most state appellate case law, and Eyecite is a mature, actively maintained citation-parsing library — both already exist and are free today, removing the historical cost barrier (licensed caselaw data) that made this previously infeasible to build cheaply. The last-30-days CaseCite Show HN shows renewed, if modest, community energy toward exactly this "free legal research" framing, but as a single-maintainer early release rather than a tool drawing on the full ecosystem of existing free pieces the gap-analyst identified.

## Skill Angle
A companion Claude Code skill triggers when a user pastes a legal question or a specific citation they want verified. The skill's inputs are the user's plain-language question or citation text (manual), and it then queries CourtListener's public API live (CONNECTED, same as the project — no persistent index is needed for a single-shot invocation). It returns 3-5 directly quoted, linked supporting cases, and flags plainly if a user-provided citation does not resolve to a real case in CourtListener. This differs from the project: the project is the maintained, cached, full-interface research app with the plain-language explanation layer for sustained pro se case research; the skill is a lightweight, one-shot citation-check or quick-lookup aid usable inside any existing chat session without standing up the full app.

## MVP Sketch
A single web app (or CLI) wired directly to CourtListener's REST API for retrieval — no need to build or maintain a separate embedding index at MVP stage, since CourtListener's own search API already handles relevance — with Eyecite post-processing citations and a strict "quote-or-refuse" generation prompt. Validate with 1-2 legal aid clinic partners running it against 20-30 real historical case questions, manually checking the citation-hallucination rate and plain-language usefulness against clinic staff judgment. Target: 2-4 weeks, 1-2 people, ideally with a legal aid clinic partner to sanity-check output.

## Risks & Open Questions
- CourtListener's coverage is thinner for some state trial courts than for federal/appellate law — the product needs to be upfront about this coverage gap rather than implying comprehensive coverage.
- Legal aid is a regulated, liability-sensitive space; this needs explicit, prominent "not legal advice" framing and likely a legal/ethics review pass before any clinic deploys it with real clients.
- The strongest reason this might already be solved: if the cited CaseCite GitHub repo gains a maintainer and momentum toward exactly this consolidation, this idea becomes redundant — worth checking CaseCite's own trajectory before investing further.
- Quote-grounding prevents hallucinated citations but not misapplication — a non-lawyer user can still be shown a correctly-quoted case that's the wrong jurisdiction or procedural posture for their situation; the plain-language layer needs an explicit "this may not apply to your situation, ask a human" escalation path, not citations alone.
