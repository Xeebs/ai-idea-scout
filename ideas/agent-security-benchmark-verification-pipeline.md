## The Gap
`icecreamyoubest/awesome-agentic-ai-security` PR #28 ("Auto update review: evidence and benchmark candidates") states plainly: "This PR was generated by the Agentic AI Security update-review pipeline. It proposes reviewable evidence changes only; maintainers should verify claims before merging." WebSearch corroborates the pattern from the vendor side: one tool "reports a 100% unsafe action prevention across 22 agentic scenarios" (self-reported, single project), and another claims "an audited 0.00% violation rate" that could not be traced to a primary source. AIRQ's third-party survey of 100 agents found 83% of claimed defenses have no independent verification at all — the "has anyone actually checked this" step is explicitly unautomated industry-wide.

## The Project
A pipeline that ingests a published agent-security benchmark claim, extracts the claimed metric, scenario count, and described test methodology, attempts to locate or reconstruct the underlying test scenarios (from a linked public dataset, a named agentic-safety benchmark suite, or by generating comparable adversarial scenarios when none is public), re-runs the vendor's tool — where it is open-source or has an accessible API — against that reconstructed scenario set in an isolated sandbox, and publishes a side-by-side "claimed vs. independently re-run" comparison with a confidence rating reflecting how much of the original methodology could be reconstructed exactly versus approximated.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: engineering and security teams evaluating which agent-security/guardrail tool to adopt, the same buyer population AIRQ's 100-agent survey covered, where 83% of claimed defenses carried zero independent verification at the time a buying decision was being made.
**Mechanism**: today a buyer either trusts a vendor's self-reported number outright or commissions a manual, accredited audit (AIUC-1-style — costly and slow, taking months). An automated re-run pipeline gives a fast, repeatable, confidence-rated second opinion on the specific published claim they're evaluating, closing the exact gap the awesome-list PR bot names as still manual, without requiring every buyer to become their own security auditor.
**Measure**: (1) the number of vendor claims the pipeline can fully or partially re-run vs. "unreconstructable" (where methodology is too opaque), as a coverage metric; (2) the delta between a vendor's claimed metric and the pipeline's independently-measured one on re-run cases — the thing buyers actually need to know; (3) time from "a vendor publishes a new claim" to "an independent re-run result exists" (target: days, not the months an accredited audit takes).

## Why Now
AIUC-1 and AIRQ both launched in 2026 as the first real market signals that buyers now demand independent verification, and the underlying agentic-safety benchmark suites and sandboxed agent-execution environments needed to actually re-run a vendor's claim are mature enough today — open benchmark datasets, containerized agent sandboxes — that automating the re-run, rather than commissioning a bespoke manual audit each time, is newly tractable.

## Skill Angle
Trigger: a buyer pastes or links one specific vendor claim they're evaluating before a purchase decision. The skill assesses whether enough methodology detail exists to meaningfully check the claim, attempts a lightweight reconstruction of the described test conditions, and flags red flags (vague scenario counts, no linked dataset, metrics that don't match the described test) as a one-shot due-diligence check — distinct from the project's full automated re-run infrastructure, which requires a sandboxed execution environment the skill doesn't have.

## MVP Sketch
Start with one well-documented self-reported claim (the "100% unsafe-action prevention across 22 agentic scenarios" example cited in the digest): manually reconstruct its 22-scenario description from the vendor's own disclosure, re-run the open-source tool against them in a sandbox, and publish the delta. Single experiment: does the independently re-run number match the claimed one, and how much of the methodology had to be approximated vs. reconstructed exactly. 3-4 weeks for 1-2 people.

## Risks & Open Questions
- Most claims won't come with enough methodology detail to reconstruct faithfully — the pipeline may spend most of its effort on claims it can only approximate, weakening comparison credibility.
- Vendors could push back hard, or threaten legal action, over public "your claim doesn't hold up" comparisons; needs a transparent, defensible, methodology-disclosure-first process.
- Safely re-running an agentic benchmark requires a genuinely isolated sandbox since the scenarios are adversarial by design — building that infrastructure correctly is itself a nontrivial, safety-relevant piece of work.
- A well-funded entrant (AIUC) or the awesome-list maintainers themselves could extend into automated re-testing once the gap is named this publicly, shrinking the addressable opportunity.
