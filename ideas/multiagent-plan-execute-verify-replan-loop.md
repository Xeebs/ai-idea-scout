## The Gap
An ICLR 2026 workshop paper reports that an explicit verifier step between a planner and its workers — one that checks whether the combined worker outputs actually answer the original query before results ship — raises answer completeness from 3.1 to 4.2 and source quality from 2.6 to 4.1 (5-point scale) on 25 expert-curated market-research queries versus trusting the orchestrator's own aggregation. A practitioner "verifier paradox" rebuttal and arkforge.tech's "Agent Blind Spots" post both argue this doesn't close the gap: an orchestrator's logs and self-checks are just claims the system makes about itself, and nobody has shipped an *independent* witness that a reasoning team's engineering org could actually adopt. The benchmark is narrow, the critique is live, and the only adjacent shipped code (VeriMAP) targets subtask-level correctness, not whole-pipeline answer completeness against the original ask.

## The Project
A verification middleware layer that sits between a multi-agent pipeline's aggregation step and whatever consumes its output. Instead of trusting the orchestrator's own summary of what its workers found, the verifier is architecturally separate: it re-reads the original query plus every worker's raw output, scores the combined result against the query on explicit, checkable axes (completeness — does every sub-question in the original ask have a traceable answer; source quality — are claims backed by retrieved evidence or hallucinated; coverage gaps — what obvious angles were never explored), and either passes the result through or triggers a bounded replan with a structured gap report (not a vague "try again"). The independence of the check — it never sees or inherits the orchestrator's own self-assessment — is the centerpiece; a verifier that reads the orchestrator's summary of itself reproduces the exact self-verification failure the "Agent Blind Spots" critique names.

Components: a query-decomposition extractor (breaks the original ask into checkable sub-questions); a per-sub-question evidence-grounding checker against worker outputs; a gap-report generator that a replanning step can consume as structured input rather than free text; adapters for LangGraph/CrewAI/OpenAI Agents SDK run-state; a replay harness to replicate the VMAO paper's own completeness/source-quality scoring on a team's own queries.

## Tangible Impact
**Population**: Teams running multi-step, multi-agent research or analysis pipelines whose output feeds a real decision — market research, competitive analysis, due diligence, and internal "deep research" tooling built on LangGraph/CrewAI/AutoGen (a combined 300k+ GitHub stars across those three frameworks is a rough proxy for how many teams are assembling orchestrator-aggregates-worker-output pipelines today with no independent completeness check).
**Mechanism**: Today, an incomplete or source-thin synthesized answer ships the moment the orchestrator's own aggregation step decides it's done — nobody downstream can tell a thorough answer from a confidently-worded gap until a human later notices a missed competitor or an unsupported claim. An independent verifier converts that silent failure into a caught, structured gap report before the answer ships, and a bounded replan closes the specific gap instead of a full, expensive re-run.
**Measure**: (1) Answer completeness and source-quality scores (same 5-point rubric as the VMAO paper) on a pilot team's own query set, with-verifier vs. without. (2) Rate of post-delivery human corrections ("this missed X") on verified vs. unverified pipeline runs, before/after adoption. (3) False-positive replan rate — how often the verifier triggers a replan on an output a human judge would have accepted as complete, to confirm the check isn't just adding cost without catching real gaps.

## Why Now
Multi-agent "deep research" pipelines moved from demo to production decision-support tooling in 2026, and the ICLR workshop paper plus the live "verifier paradox" debate show the field has validated that self-aggregation misses real gaps but hasn't agreed on, let alone shipped, an independent check — the demand and the first evidence both just appeared in the same 30-day window.

## Skill Angle
Given a pasted original query, the planner's task breakdown, and the raw output from each worker agent, decompose the query into checkable sub-questions, flag which ones have no traceable answer in the worker outputs, flag claims with no cited evidence among the outputs, and produce a short structured gap report (missing sub-answers, unsupported claims, unexplored angles) a human or a replanning step can act on directly. This is useful standalone the moment someone has a completed multi-agent run they want audited, independent of wiring the full middleware into their pipeline.

## MVP Sketch
A Python library that hooks into one framework's run-state (LangGraph first), extracts sub-questions from the original query via a single LLM call, checks each worker output for grounded evidence per sub-question, and emits a gap report plus a pass/replan decision. Experiment: re-run the VMAO paper's own 25-query benchmark (or a comparable team-authored set) through a single-agent-aggregation baseline and the verifier-gated pipeline, and compare completeness/source-quality scores; if the verifier doesn't move the numbers on a held-out query set, the independence claim is unproven and the idea should be shelved. 3–4 weeks for 1–2 people.

## Risks & Open Questions
- The "verifier paradox" critique applies to this build too if the verifier is itself an LLM call with no grounding mechanism distinct from the orchestrator's own reasoning — the differentiation has to be the evidence-grounding check, not just "ask a second model."
- A narrow academic benchmark (25 queries) may not generalize; the MVP experiment needs a second, team-authored query set to avoid overfitting to the paper's own evaluation.
- Megagon's VeriMAP already covers subtask-level verification functions — if a team's real gap is subtask correctness rather than whole-pipeline completeness, VeriMAP may already be the better fit and this project would need to differentiate clearly on the whole-answer axis.
- Replan loops that are too eager add latency/cost without improving quality; the false-positive measure above is load-bearing, not optional.
